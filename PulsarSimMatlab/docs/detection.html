Detection and good TOAs: detectPulsar validated

A TOA fit always finds a peak, even in pure noise. detectPulsar asks the separate question "is the pulsar there?" at a fixed false-alarm probability. Together with the χ² flag it decides which TOAs are good.

[detection, info] = detectPulsar(fold, info_fold, template, 'Bnoise', Bnoise, 'Weighting', weighting)

Full interface and help text of detectPulsar →

In short
  1. Normalize the folded profile by its noise if there were no pulsar (H0), using only the profile and the fold's weights.
  2. Match it with the template: at the predicted phase (T0), and at every possible shift (Tmax).
  3. Compare with a threshold that noise alone exceeds with probability 10⁻³: 3.09 at a known phase, 4.15 when all shifts are searched.
  4. Good TOA = fitted, detected at unknown phase, and χ² not flagged.

The question

detectPulsar makes a Neyman–Pearson test of two hypotheses: H0 (baseline + noise) against H1 (baseline + template-shaped pulse + noise). It tests at a fixed false-alarm probability PFA, by default 10⁻³ per profile, for every sub-int and for the total fold. It uses only what the observer has: the fold, the template, Bnoise and PFA.

H0 noise normalization

Under H0 the profile is flat at the baseline a = mean(p); under H0 that is exact. Each time bin then has variance a²/(Bn·Δt). Each phase bin has vj = that·weight2j/weightj², and the neighbour covariances come from weightX, as in estimateTOA. normProfile = (p − a)/√v is the profile in noise units.

Known phase: T0

\[ T_0 = \frac{c^{\mathsf T}(p - a)}{\sqrt{c^{\mathsf T}\, \mathrm{Cov}\; c}} \]
\(T_0\)
the known-phase test statistic, in units of its noise
\(p\)
the profile as a vector over phase bins
\(a\)
baseline: mean of \(p\)
\(c\)
the template at the predicted phase, minus its mean
\(\mathrm{Cov}\)
covariance matrix of the profile bins under H0 (noise only), from the radiometer model and the fold weights

T0 is a weighted sum of Gaussian bins, divided by its exact standard deviation. Under H0 it is therefore exactly N(0, 1), and the threshold and the detection probability are:

\[ \eta_0 = \Phi^{-1}(1 - P_\mathrm{FA}) = 3.09 \text{ for } P_\mathrm{FA} = 10^{-3}, \qquad P_D = 1 - \Phi(\eta - \mathrm{SNR}) \]
\(\eta_0\)
detection threshold on \(T_0\) (etaKnown)
\(\Phi\)
cumulative distribution function of the standard normal distribution (not the phase \(\Phi_0\))
\(P_\mathrm{FA}\)
false-alarm probability (PFA, default 10⁻³)
\(P_D\)
detection probability for a pulse with this SNR
\(\eta\)
the threshold used (\(\eta_0\) here, or the unknown-phase one below)

The predicted phase is the ephemeris phase ('Phase', default 0). This is the tracking case: you know your position and clock well enough to predict where the pulse is, so one test suffices.

Unknown phase: Tmax and Rice's formula

Acquisition, or a cold start: the position is uncertain by more than a pulse width. (1 ms of timing is 300 km; one J0437 turn is about 1700 km.) So the pulse could be anywhere. T(τ) is computed for every bin shift, by FFT correlation of both its numerator and its variance. Tmax is the largest of them, at phaseMax. Searching gives noise more chances, so it needs a higher threshold.

How much higher? Neighbouring shifts are nearly identical, because a smooth template moved by one bin barely changes. So "number of bins × Q(η)" would be far too pessimistic. Rice (1944) gives the expected number of up-crossings of a level η by a smooth, stationary Gaussian process. The maximum exceeds η if T starts above it, or crosses it:

\[ P(T_\mathrm{max} > \eta) \approx Q(\eta) + \frac{\sqrt{\lambda_2}}{2\pi}\, e^{-\eta^2/2}, \qquad \lambda_2 = \frac{\sum_{k \ge 1} (2\pi k)^2\, |S_k|^2}{\sum_{k \ge 1} |S_k|^2} \]
\(T_\mathrm{max}\)
the largest \(T(\tau)\) over all shifts (Tmax)
\(T(\tau)\)
the test statistic \(T_0\) with the template shifted by \(\tau\) turns
\(\eta\)
threshold (etaUnknown)
\(Q(\eta)\)
standard-normal tail probability, \(1 - \Phi(\eta)\)
\(S_k\)
\(k\)-th Fourier coefficient of the template; \(k\) = harmonic (cycles per turn)
\(\lambda_2\)
second spectral moment of \(T(\tau)\) [turns⁻²]: how fast it varies with \(\tau\) (info.lambda2)
\(\sqrt{\lambda_2}/2\pi\)
the Rice factor (info.riceFactor)

For the 5 % Gaussian template, the "Rice factor" √λ₂/2π is 5.51: the mean number of up-crossings of the mean level per turn. Solving for PFA = 10⁻³ (with fzero) gives η = 4.15.

In terms of independent trials, PFA/Q(η) ≈ 61 at that threshold. So the phase search costs as much as about 60 independent tests, not 5.5. (5.5 independent trials would give η = 3.57.) In the tail, crossings are rare, so the approximation is tight.

T0 vs Tmax, and detection vs χ²

  • T0 is the matched-filter value at the predicted phase: one test, N(0,1) under H0.
  • Tmax is the largest value over all shifts: under H0 typically 1.5–2 even with no pulsar.
  • With a pulsar and a correct ephemeris, T0 ≈ Tmax ≈ SNR and phaseMax ≈ the pulse phase.

Detection catches "nothing there" (noise-peak outliers); χ² catches "something wrong there".

Why the unknown-phase detection

FFTFIT itself searches all phases for the highest peak, so the fair question is "is the best peak anywhere real?", which is the unknown-phase test. Using the known-phase test would accept TOAs whose fitted peak is a noise peak elsewhere.

The result, from the threshold sweep: the good TOAs are free of outliers at every SNR tested. What fraction of the TOAs is good depends on the SNR per sub-int:

Noise check

noiseRatio is the off-pulse variance divided by the radiometer variance with the off-pulse baseline. (Off-pulse means where the template at phaseMax is below 1 % of its peak.) It is about 1 when the noise model is right. A carrier gives about 1.65 on the full band, and a radar far more. Measured: 0.992 at −5 dB, and a median of 0.996 over 900 noise-only profiles.

Channels

Equal weighting. As in estimateTOA, the test uses the sum of the channel profiles, with the exclusion rule (combineChannels). The H0 noise of each channel is taken at its own baseline ac, with all lags.

Optimal weighting (used by main.m). The test is the weighted matched filter over channels:

\[ N(\tau) = \sum_c s_c \sum_j w_{cj}\, (p_{cj} - \bar p_c)\; T(\phi_j - \tau), \qquad w_{cj} = \frac{1}{\operatorname{var}(p_{cj})} \text{ under H0} \]
\(N(\tau)\)
numerator of the matched filter at shift \(\tau\) [turns]
\(c\), \(j\)
channel index; phase-bin index
\(s_c\)
known relative gain of channel \(c\) (ChannelGain)
\(p_{cj}\), \(\bar p_c\)
profile of channel \(c\); its mean (the H0 baseline \(a_c\))
\(w_{cj}\)
weight: one over the bin's noise variance under H0
\(T\), \(\phi_j\)
the template (not the period); the phase of bin \(j\)

It is normalized by its exact H0 standard deviation, including all lags of weightX. So T0 and T(τ) stay N(0,1) under H0, and the thresholds, 3.09 and 4.15, are unchanged. All shifts are computed at once, with one FFT batch per lag. There is no exclusion rule.

A subtlety

Rice's formula assumes a stationary process; with blanking the weights vary slightly with phase. The Monte Carlo below measured the false-alarm rate as designed anyway.

Validation

  • NumPy, 20,000 H0 profiles with neighbour correlation: T0 mean 0.014, std 0.996; P(Tmax > η) 0.0098 at 10⁻² and 0.0009 at 10⁻³.
  • Long H0 run (runH0.m, 900 noise-only profiles): the standard deviation of T0 is 1.008 ± 0.024. The false alarms match the expected numbers:

  • SNR sweep: 0 false alarms in 90 profiles under a true H0. The mean of T0 equals the matched-filter SNR in the weak regime, and PD follows the theory curves.

An H0 Monte Carlo gives false-alarm rates of 0.054 (known phase) and 0.051 (unknown phase), against a design of 0.05 ± 0.008. Under H1 the detected fraction is 0.914, against 0.910 in theory. Optimal weighting detects with 1.041 × the SNR of equal weighting.

Baseline-estimation loss

T0 ≈ 0.96 × the theoretical SNR. The theory assumes a known noise level; the observer estimates the baseline from the same profile, which discards the template's DC part: loss = √(1 − (Σs)²/(N·Σs²)) = 0.962 for the 5 % Gaussian. Real physics: any observer without a known baseline pays it.

Limits

PFA is validated down to 10⁻²…10⁻³. Smaller values (e.g. 10⁻⁶ for navigation) rely on extrapolating the Gaussian and Rice tails. At high SNR T0 exceeds the SNR (self-noise is ignored in the H0 normalization); irrelevant for weak signals.
