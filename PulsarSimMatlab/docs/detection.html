Detection and good TOAs: detectPulsar validated

A TOA fit always finds a peak, even in pure noise. detectPulsar asks the separate question "is the pulsar there?" at a fixed false-alarm probability. Together with the χ² flag it decides which TOAs are good.

[detection, info] = detectPulsar(fold, info_fold, template, 'Bnoise', Bnoise, 'Weighting', weighting)

Full interface and help text of detectPulsar →

The question

Neyman–Pearson test of H0 (baseline + noise) against H1 (baseline + template-shaped pulse + noise) at a fixed false-alarm probability PFA (default 10⁻³ per profile), for every sub-int and for the total fold. Observer-only inputs: fold, template, Bnoise, PFA.

H0 noise normalization

Under H0 the profile is flat at the baseline a = mean(p) (exact under H0). Per time bin var = a²/(Bn·Δt); per phase bin vj = that·weight2j/weightj², neighbour covariances from weightX, as in estimateTOA. normProfile = (p − a)/√v: the profile in noise units.

Known phase: T0

T0 = cᵀ(p − a) / √(cᵀ·Cov·c),   c = template at the predicted phase minus its mean

A weighted sum of Gaussian bins divided by its exact standard deviation: under H0 exactly N(0, 1), so

η0 = Φ⁻¹(1 − PFA) = 3.09 for 10⁻³,   PD = 1 − Φ(η − SNR)

The predicted phase is the ephemeris phase ('Phase', default 0). This is the tracking case: you know your position and clock well enough to predict where the pulse is, so one test suffices.

Unknown phase: Tmax and Rice's formula

Acquisition / cold start: the position is uncertain by more than a pulse width (1 ms of timing is 300 km; one J0437 turn is ~1700 km), so the pulse could be anywhere. T(τ) is computed for every bin shift by FFT correlation (numerator and variance), and Tmax = max T(τ) at phaseMax. More chances for noise need a higher threshold.

Neighbouring shifts are nearly identical (a smooth template moved by one bin), so "number of bins × Q(η)" would be far too pessimistic. Rice (1944) gives the expected number of upcrossings of level η by a smooth stationary Gaussian process; the maximum exceeds η if T starts above it or crosses it:

P(Tmax > η) ≈ Q(η) + (√λ₂/2π)·e−η²/2,   λ₂ = Σ(2πk)²|Sk|² / Σ|Sk|² (k ≥ 1)

For the 5 % Gaussian template √λ₂/2π = 5.51 (the "Rice factor", roughly the number of independent phase trials per turn), and η = 4.15 at PFA = 10⁻³ (solved with fzero). In the tail, crossings are rare, so the approximation is tight.

T0 vs Tmax, and detection vs χ²

  • T0 is the matched-filter value at the predicted phase: one test, N(0,1) under H0.
  • Tmax is the largest value over all shifts: under H0 typically 1.5–2 even with no pulsar.
  • With a pulsar and a correct ephemeris, T0 ≈ Tmax ≈ SNR and phaseMax ≈ the pulse phase.

Detection catches "nothing there" (noise-peak outliers); χ² catches "something wrong there".

Good TOAs (in main.m)

toa.good = toa.valid ∧ detection.detectedUnknown ∧ ¬toa.flagChi2
Why the unknown-phase detection

FFTFIT itself searches all phases for the highest peak, so the fair question is "is the best peak anywhere real?", which is the unknown-phase test. Using the known-phase test would accept TOAs whose fitted peak is a noise peak elsewhere.

Result (threshold sweep, 6 Oct): good TOAs are outlier-free at every SNR tested; at SNR 3.8 per sub-int 40 % survive, at 6.0 92 %, at 7.5 100 %. This is where the design rule SNR ≥ 6–7 per sub-int comes from.

Noise check

noiseRatio = off-pulse variance (template at phaseMax < 1 % of peak) / the radiometer variance with the off-pulse baseline. ≈ 1 when the noise model is right; a carrier gives ~1.65 on the full band, a radar ≫ 1. Measured 0.992 at −5 dB, median 0.996 over 900 noise-only profiles.

Channels

Equal weighting (A2b, 7 Oct): as in estimateTOA, the sum of channel profiles with the exclusion rule (combineChannels); H0 noise per channel at its own baseline ac; all lags.

Optimal weighting (A2c-2, 8 Oct; used by main.m): the weighted matched filter over channels,

N(τ) = Σc sc Σj wcj(pcj − p̄c)·T(φj − τ),   w = 1/var under H0

normalized by its exact H0 standard deviation including all lags of weightX, so T0 and T(τ) stay N(0,1) under H0 and the thresholds (3.09 / 4.15) are unchanged. All shifts at once with one FFT batch per lag. No exclusion rule.

A subtlety

Rice's formula assumes a stationary process; with blanking the weights vary slightly with phase. The Monte Carlo below measured the false-alarm rate as designed anyway.

Validation

  • NumPy, 20,000 H0 profiles with neighbour correlation: T0 mean 0.014, std 0.996; P(Tmax > η) 0.0098 at 10⁻² and 0.0009 at 10⁻³.
  • Long H0 run (runH0.m, 900 noise-only profiles): T0 std 1.008 ± 0.024; exceedances at PFA 10⁻¹/10⁻²/10⁻³: T0 96/7/3, Tmax 88/13/1 (expected 90/9/0.9).
  • Phase E sweep: 0/90 false alarms under a true H0; T0 mean = matched-filter SNR in the weak regime; PD on the theory curves.
  • tests/testChannelTOA.m 4–6 and tests/testOptimalTOA.m 6–10: bit-identical defaults; T0, Tmax, normProfile = explicit full H0 covariance formulas to ≤ 10⁻¹⁵; H0 Monte Carlo false alarms 0.054 known / 0.051 unknown (design 0.05 ± 0.008); H1 detected fraction 0.914 vs theory 0.910; optimal/equal detection SNR 1.041.
Baseline-estimation loss

T0 ≈ 0.96 × the theoretical SNR. The theory assumes a known noise level; the observer estimates the baseline from the same profile, which discards the template's DC part: loss = √(1 − (Σs)²/(N·Σs²)) = 0.962 for the 5 % Gaussian. Real physics: any observer without a known baseline pays it.

Limits

PFA is validated down to 10⁻²…10⁻³. Smaller values (e.g. 10⁻⁶ for navigation) rely on extrapolating the Gaussian and Rice tails. At high SNR T0 exceeds the SNR (self-noise is ignored in the H0 normalization); irrelevant for weak signals.
