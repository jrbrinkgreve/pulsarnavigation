Limitations and modelling gaps

What the simulation leaves out, what the processing assumes without checking, and how big each effect is. Each item says whether it matters for the weak-signal target (−54 dB, J0437 on a 0.61 m² array).

In short

Over the 0.1–1 s it simulates, the pipeline is exact for what it models. What it leaves out matters on longer timescales. Four effects are larger than a J0437 TOA and must be handled before folding for hours: Earth's motion, the pulsar's binary orbit, the clock and the station position (block D). Others only matter for nanosecond navigation: the ionosphere, DM changes, scattering at high DM. The receiver model is ideal, and two excision items are parked with designs.

How big each effect is

Processing assumptions not checked

  • DM correctness: a wrong DM leaves a residual sweep. A DM check from sub-band TOAs is planned (block 2).
  • Reference-frequency convention vs the TOA definition: a constant offset if the forward and inverse references differ.
  • Receiver frequency mapping: no spectral inversion assumed (fRF = fLO + fbb); the off-centre LO makes a mirror error visible in simulation, but real hardware must be checked.
  • True sample rate: trusted via the info chain.

How scintillation would be modelled

Within a ≤ 1 s voltage simulation, diffractive scintillation is static: a random frequency gain g(f) with correlation bandwidth Δνd multiplied into the forward filter, plus optionally a multipath tail. Long-term fading, Doppler and DM(t) belong at the TOA / SNR level of a later mission simulation. With a scintillated spectrum, optimal detection weights channels by pulsar S/N (ChannelGain in the optimal fit).

Signal and receiver

  • Realistic SNR regime. −5 dB is a bright pulsar on a large dish. A small antenna (SEFD ~10⁵ Jy) gives ρ ≈ −27 dB (Vela) to ≲ −50 dB (typical millisecond pulsars): per-pulse SNR ≪ 1, TOAs need minutes to hours of folding, where motion, DM(t) and scintillation matter.
  • Spectrum: the generator is white; real pulsars have S ∝ fα, α ≈ −1.6 (factor 1.6 across 1.2–1.6 GHz). Equal-weight loss ~1 %; with profile evolution and a DM error it biases wideband TOAs.
  • Receiver: ideal linear filters, infinite dynamic range. No amplifier compression, ADC quantization or clipping (2-bit costs ≈ 12 % SNR), intermodulation, bandpass ripple, gain drift. Single polarization (dual pol gives √2 in SNR; the hardware is dual pol). Ideal I/Q (a digital downconverter): no I/Q imbalance (a mirror image, −25…−40 dB before correction), no DC offset or LO leakage, no LO phase noise. Our LO (1.3 GHz) lies inside the band, so a DC spike and imbalance ghosts (RFI at 1.35 GHz → ghost at 1.25 GHz) would land in it; real hardware needs a low-IF LO or imbalance calibration.
  • Receiver noise does not pass the dispersion taper in the simulation (only the pulsar is tapered at the band edges): +0.4 % in σTOA on the full band, but +2.5 % on the default channel path. There the pulsar sees the band-edge taper and every channel's edges, while the noise sees only the channel edges, and the six edge channels hold 2–96 % of the pulse although the optimal fit assumes equal pulse strength (ChannelGain 1). This is the whole 2.2 % gap between the channel path's and the full band's error bars (2.943 vs 2.880 µs; predicted +2.13 %, measured +2.19 %). With a realistic receiver (bandpass on pulsar and noise) the channel path would cost 0.37 % (logs/2026-10-13_deep-analysis.md, section 2).
  • Real bandpass is not ideal and not known exactly: measure W² from the off-pulse spectrum and use it in (∫W²)²/∫W⁴; can be 10–30 % on real data.

Timing and pulse

  • No clock jitter or drift. A clock rate off by 10⁻⁹ accumulates 72 µs over 20 h, ~6× the J0437 TOA precision. A constant rate can be fitted; random wander cannot. Belongs in the phase predictor (D); "what oscillator does the hardware have?" is an open question.
  • No relative motion. Earth's orbital v/c ≈ 10⁻⁴ → ≈ 100 µs per second of phase drift at 100 Hz. That drift is the navigation signal; it needs Doppler terms in the fold phase model (block D, a hard requirement for multi-day folds).
  • No binary motion of the pulsar (found 13 Oct, deep analysis section 14). The reference pulsar J0437−4715 is in a 5.74-day orbit (projected size 3.37 light-seconds): its orbit moves the arrival time by ~3 s within one 20 h fold (0.15 s per hour), ~2·10⁵× the fold's phase budget (W50/10 ≈ 14 µs). J2145−0750 and J1713+0747 are binaries too. The phase predictor (D) needs a binary timing model (circular-orbit ELL1 suffices for J0437 at the µs level), and end-to-end tests of D need binary motion in the simulator.
  • Position prior for folding. A station position error δr gives a daily-varying delay error up to δr/c, which smears a long fold: J0437 needs the position to ≲ 4 km, wide-pulse normal pulsars tolerate ~100 km. A fix from scratch starts from the normal pulsars and refines with J0437.
  • Navigation expectations (rough, from the scenario numbers, no timing noise): a first 4-pulsar fix from 30° S (J0437, Vela, B0950+08, B1749−28) is ~300 km (3-D), limited by the normal pulsars' 0.3–0.7 ms TOAs, after 184 h on source worst case (34 h nominal), ~2–3 weeks of calendar time with visibility; from 52° N (no MSP) ~1500 km after weeks to months. See scenario.
  • Single pulsar.
  • Stable profile: no pulse-to-pulse jitter, no profile evolution with frequency, no polarization, no mode changes (B0329+54: abnormal mode ~15 % of the time). Since 20 Oct the fast simulator and the TOA code take multi-component profiles (B0329+54's five Gaussians at 1410 MHz; a single Gaussian with the catalogue W50 overstated its peak by 3 dB, and its TOA budget by 3×); the voltage-chain generator and expectedPowerModel are still single-Gaussian, and J0437's wings (W10 1.02 ms vs W50 0.14 ms) are not modelled yet.

Estimation and detection

  • FFTFIT is not optimal under self-noise (1.5× at +10 dB, 2.4× at +20 dB); irrelevant at weak signal.
  • Below SNR ≈ 6 per sub-int TOAs have outliers; the good-TOA definition removes them.
  • Error bars at SNR ~7 (found 20 Oct, under investigation). The formal σ uses the fitted amplitude, so at low SNR it is too small when noise inflates the amplitude and too large when it deflates it (scenario runs: pull width 0.7 below the median fitted SNR, 1.1–1.4 above). Overall honest for single Gaussians (B0329+54 1.04, J0437 0.87–0.94), a fat tail with a five-component template (1.18, robust 1.01, one TOA at 6.9σ). Fixed sub-ints at SNR 5–40 separate two effects: (1) the five-component template makes the error bars too small at low SNR whatever the grouping (pull width 1.82 at SNR 5, 1.19 at 7, 1.09 at 10, 1.03 at 20), while single Gaussians are honest from SNR 7 (0.97–1.10); (2) J0437's narrow pulls were a statistical fluctuation, not an effect: its two scenario runs give 0.94 and 0.87 (the robust width of ~110 TOAs scatters by ±0.16), and adaptive and fixed groups on the same simulated data both give ~1.1 ± 0.07. Most of (1) is bin resolution: B0329+54's narrowest component (FWHM 0.0055 turns) is 5.6 bins wide at NBin 1024 — within estimateTOA's rule (bins ≤ FWHM/5), but at low SNR that costs more than the rule assumed: with NBin 2048 (11 bins) the pull width at SNR 7 drops from 1.18 to 1.08 ± 0.03, the ~5–10 % that single Gaussians show at SNR 7 too. Fix: NBin 2048 for B0329+54 (narrowest component ≥ ~10 bins).
  • PFA validated down to 10⁻²…10⁻³; smaller values (10⁻⁶ for navigation) by extrapolating Gaussian / Rice tails.
  • Covariance between consecutive sub-ints not stored (~1–4 % of the variance of 2–3 off-pulse bins at the boundary; negligible).
  • Blanking: V and X use white input (error bars only, within 1–2 % in heavily blanked bins). Only the first 7 lags of X are stored; near a blank 0.4–0.7 % of a bin's variance (up to ~1.6 %) lies at longer lags (error bars only).

RFI excision

See RFI excision: limits: data-dependent truncation (−0.4 % pulse amplitude at −5 dB), bright pulsars (above −5 dB, or noise-free) blanking themselves (main.m warns), residual bias from an exactly locked radar (0.41 µs at −54 dB with the frequency guard and the periodic mask), the periodic mask for fixed PRFs only (staggered PRFs not yet), carrier/GNSS channels with high baselines, windows ≤ 16 samples, noise-like RFI undetectable, assumed 0.1 µs radar rise time.

Flat spectrum within a channel (found 8 Oct, B5d)

detectRFI's thresholds and the fold's noise model (V, X) assume each channel's noise spectrum is flat (the channelizer's). A channel only partly covered by strong wideband RFI — the band edges of an LTE carrier — has a lopsided spectrum: its samples stay correlated over microseconds, so a window holds fewer independent samples than assumed. Result at LTE 0 dB: false flags in the two edge channels (540 and 367 windows vs ~3), and detected-power noise above the model (red. χ² 1.04, so slightly too small TOA error bars). Possible remedies: a per-channel noise level measured on the data, per-channel thresholds from the measured spectrum, or flagging such edge channels whole.

Measured 9 Oct (B5e, results): the TOA fit weights a channel by 1/level², and these channels contain the RFI, so χ² overstates the effect: error bars 0.08 % too small at LTE 0 dB, 0.39 % in the realistic scenario. The worst case is a narrow noise-like carrier about as strong as its channel's noise: with ten such (Inmarsat-like) carriers χ² 1.22 and error bars 3.4 % too small. → Per-channel noise calibration, implemented 20 Oct as a switch (noiseCal, off by default; synthetic checks pass, chain check next): the observation's own off-pulse noise per channel replaces the flat-channel model.

Periodic mask: false emitters when detections are dense (found 9 Oct, B5e)

periodicRFI looks for a fixed rhythm in the detections and assumes the other detections are few and randomly timed. In the realistic scenario the lopsided channels give hundreds of false flags each. In that dense cloud it accepted rhythms that are not there (14.1 kHz: every second pulse of a real 973 Hz radar plus random flags; 14.5 kHz: detectRFI's window steps put all detection times on a 4-sample grid, which the chance calculation ignores) and blanked every predicted "pulse" — every ~70 µs across ~100 channels: 29–32 % of the data (SNR −15 %). Nothing checks physical plausibility (only ~5 % of these "radars'" predicted pulses were detected, ≥ 40 % for real ones). The periodic mask is therefore off by default since 9 Oct (it is only needed for radars locked to the pulsar). Fix designed, not implemented: a grid-aware chance calculation, a minimum fraction of predicted pulses detected, and trial periods from strong pulses (block-B handover 2026-10-09_handover-B-closed.md).

Edges and scale

  • The last ~6.3 ms of each file are not fully supported; partial first and last turns are skipped (MinCoverage = 1).
  • High DM in the generator: forward dispersion needs ~47 GB from DM ~54 up to DM ~106, so at both DM 60 and DM 100 (24 GB machine). The processing side is solved by the channelized front end; the generator side is open (roadmap).
  • Disk per second of simulated data: ~61 GB on the full band, ~63 GB on the channel path, ~72 GB with excision (blanked copies and the weight file). Hours of data need the fast simulator (block C).
  • Excision on long files (12 Oct): the front end streams, but detectRFI (reads each channel whole), periodicRFI (all events at once) and blankingWeights (each blanked channel whole; weight array 4.8 GB at L = 1 s, above its 4 GB default) do not. Fine for 0.1 s simulations and for block C; needed for real data. Designed (time blocks with the filter reach as overlap), not built.
