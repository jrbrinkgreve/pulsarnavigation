Limitations and modelling gaps

What the simulation leaves out, what the processing assumes without checking, and how big each effect is. Each item says whether it matters for the weak-signal target (−54 dB, J0437 on a 0.61 m² array).

Processing assumptions not checked

  • DM correctness: a wrong DM leaves a residual sweep. A DM check from sub-band TOAs is planned (block 2).
  • Reference-frequency convention vs the TOA definition: a constant offset if the forward and inverse references differ.
  • Receiver frequency mapping: no spectral inversion assumed (fRF = fLO + fbb); the off-centre LO makes a mirror error visible in simulation, but real hardware must be checked.
  • True sample rate: trusted via the info chain.

How scintillation would be modelled

Within a ≤ 1 s voltage simulation, diffractive scintillation is static: a random frequency gain g(f) with correlation bandwidth Δνd multiplied into the forward filter, plus optionally a multipath tail. Long-term fading, Doppler and DM(t) belong at the TOA / SNR level of a later mission simulation. With a scintillated spectrum, optimal detection weights channels by pulsar S/N (ChannelGain in the optimal fit).

Signal and receiver

  • Realistic SNR regime. −5 dB is a bright pulsar on a large dish. A small antenna (SEFD ~10⁵ Jy) gives ρ ≈ −27 dB (Vela) to ≲ −50 dB (typical millisecond pulsars): per-pulse SNR ≪ 1, TOAs need minutes to hours of folding, where motion, DM(t) and scintillation matter.
  • Spectrum: the generator is white; real pulsars have S ∝ fα, α ≈ −1.6 (factor 1.6 across 1.2–1.6 GHz). Equal-weight loss ~1 %; with profile evolution and a DM error it biases wideband TOAs.
  • Receiver: ideal linear filters, infinite dynamic range. No amplifier compression, ADC quantization or clipping (2-bit costs ≈ 12 % SNR), intermodulation, bandpass ripple, gain drift. Single polarization (dual pol gives √2 in SNR; the hardware is dual pol).
  • Receiver noise does not pass the dispersion taper in the simulation: < 1 % in SNR.
  • Real bandpass is not ideal and not known exactly: measure W² from the off-pulse spectrum and use it in (∫W²)²/∫W⁴; can be 10–30 % on real data.

Timing and pulse

  • No clock jitter or drift. A clock rate off by 10⁻⁹ accumulates 72 µs over 20 h, ~6× the J0437 TOA precision. A constant rate can be fitted; random wander cannot. Belongs in the phase predictor (D); "what oscillator does the hardware have?" is an open question.
  • No relative motion. Earth's orbital v/c ≈ 10⁻⁴ → ≈ 100 µs per second of phase drift at 100 Hz. That drift is the navigation signal; it needs Doppler terms in the fold phase model (block D, a hard requirement for multi-day folds).
  • Single pulsar.
  • Stable Gaussian profile: no pulse-to-pulse jitter, no profile evolution with frequency, no polarization. Places to revisit for real profiles: ephem.profileFWHM (→ a template per pulsar), the generator and expectedPowerModel profile, the 1 %-of-peak on-pulse threshold in estimateTOA.

Estimation and detection

  • FFTFIT is not optimal under self-noise (1.5× at +10 dB, 2.4× at +20 dB); irrelevant at weak signal.
  • Below SNR ≈ 6 per sub-int TOAs have outliers; the good-TOA definition removes them.
  • PFA validated down to 10⁻²…10⁻³; smaller values (10⁻⁶ for navigation) by extrapolating Gaussian / Rice tails.
  • Covariance between consecutive sub-ints not stored (~1–4 % of the variance of 2–3 off-pulse bins at the boundary; negligible).
  • Blanking: V and X use white input (error bars only, within 1–2 % in heavily blanked bins).

RFI excision

See RFI excision: limits: data-dependent truncation (−0.4 % pulse amplitude at −5 dB), residual bias from an exactly locked radar (0.41 µs at −54 dB with the frequency guard and the periodic mask), the periodic mask for fixed PRFs only (staggered PRFs not yet), carrier/GNSS channels with high baselines, windows ≤ 16 samples, noise-like RFI undetectable, assumed 0.1 µs radar rise time.

Flat spectrum within a channel (found 8 Oct, B5d)

detectRFI's thresholds and the fold's noise model (V, X) assume each channel's noise spectrum is flat (the channelizer's). A channel only partly covered by strong wideband RFI — the band edges of an LTE carrier — has a lopsided spectrum: its samples stay correlated over microseconds, so a window holds fewer independent samples than assumed. Result at LTE 0 dB: false flags in the two edge channels (540 and 367 windows vs ~3), and detected-power noise above the model (red. χ² 1.04, so slightly too small TOA error bars). Possible remedies: a per-channel noise level measured on the data, per-channel thresholds from the measured spectrum, or flagging such edge channels whole.

Measured 9 Oct (B5e, results): the TOA fit weights a channel by 1/level², and these channels contain the RFI, so χ² overstates the effect: error bars 0.08 % too small at LTE 0 dB, 0.39 % in the realistic scenario. The worst case is a narrow noise-like carrier about as strong as its channel's noise: with ten such (Inmarsat-like) carriers χ² 1.22 and error bars 3.4 % too small. → The per-channel noise calibration is planned (after the periodic-mask fix below).

Periodic mask: false emitters when detections are dense (found 9 Oct, B5e)

periodicRFI looks for a fixed rhythm in the detections and assumes the other detections are few and randomly timed. In the realistic scenario the lopsided channels give hundreds of false flags each. In that dense cloud it accepted rhythms that are not there (14.1 kHz: every second pulse of a real 973 Hz radar plus random flags; 14.5 kHz: probably detectRFI's window grid) and blanked every predicted "pulse" — every ~70 µs across ~100 channels: 29–32 % of the data on main's default path (SNR −15 %). Nothing checks physical plausibility (these "radars" would transmit 3–14 % of the time). Fix next (design note first): a plausibility check, the rhythm search on strong detections only, and a chance calculation that holds for dense detections.

Edges and scale

  • The last ~6.3 ms of each file are not fully supported; partial first and last turns are skipped (MinCoverage = 1).
  • High DM in the generator: forward dispersion needs ~47 GB at DM 60 and ~94 GB at DM 100 (24 GB machine). The processing side is solved by the channelized front end; the generator side is open (roadmap).
  • Disk: ~61 GB per second of simulated data. Hours of data need the fast simulator (block C).
