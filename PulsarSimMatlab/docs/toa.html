Stage 8 · TOA estimation: estimateTOA validated

Fit a template to each folded profile: the shift gives the time of arrival, and a noise model that uses only the fitted profile and the fold's weights gives an honest uncertainty. On the channel path all channels are fitted together with inverse-variance weights.

[toa, info] = estimateTOA(fold, info_fold, template, 'Bnoise', Bnoise, 'Weighting', weighting)

In short: what estimateTOA does, step by step
  1. Correlate each folded profile with the template, in the Fourier domain, so that any shift can be tried, not only whole bins.
  2. Find the peak: a coarse search on a fine grid, then Newton steps to 10⁻¹⁴ turns. The shift τ is the TOA's offset from the ephemeris.
  3. Error bar: how much the noise moves the peak, from a noise model that uses only the fitted profile and the fold's weights.
  4. Quality flag: χ² of the residuals on the pulse, which catches profiles that are not template-shaped.
  5. Channels: on the channel path all 128 channels are fitted at once, each weighted by how noisy it is.

1. The model and the TOA definition

\[ p(\phi) = a + b\, s(\phi - \tau) + \text{noise} \]
\(p(\phi)\)
the folded profile at rotational phase \(\phi\) [power units]
\(\phi\)
rotational phase from the ephemeris [turns]
\(s\)
the template: peak 1 at phase 0 (gaussianTemplate)
\(a\)
baseline (receiver-noise level)
\(b\)
pulse amplitude
\(\tau\)
phase offset of the pulse from the ephemeris prediction [turns]
\[ \mathrm{TOA} = t_\mathrm{ref} + \frac{\tau}{f_\mathrm{ref}} \]
\(\mathrm{TOA}\)
the arrival time of template phase 0 at the dedispersion reference frequency (1.6 GHz) [s] (toa.toa)
\(t_\mathrm{ref}\)
the time where the phase model reaches the sub-int's reference turn [s] (fold.subint.tRef)
\(f_\mathrm{ref}\)
the spin frequency at \(t_\mathrm{ref}\) [Hz] (fold.subint.fRef), not the 1.6 GHz radio frequency

toa.total (the whole fold) reports only the offset \(\tau/f_0\) relative to the ephemeris (\(f_0\): spin frequency), not an absolute TOA.

2. FFTFIT: finding the shift (Taylor 1992)

  1. Cross-spectrum \(X_k = P_k\, S_k^*\) for harmonics \(k = 1 \ldots \lfloor N/2 \rfloor - 1\) (DC carries only the baseline; Nyquist is ambiguous). Then
    \[ C(\tau) = \mathrm{Re} \sum_{k} X_k\, e^{\,i 2\pi k \tau} \]
    \(P_k\), \(S_k\)
    \(k\)-th Fourier coefficient (DFT) of the profile and of the template; \(^*\) is the complex conjugate
    \(k\)
    harmonic number (cycles per turn)
    \(N\)
    number of phase bins (NBin = 2048)
    \(C(\tau)\)
    the continuous cross-correlation of profile and template at shift \(\tau\) [turns]
    A shift is a phase ramp in the Fourier domain, so any sub-bin τ is exact.
  2. Coarse search: zero-padded inverse FFT on 8×N points (1/8192 turn = 1.22 µs).
  3. Refine: Newton on C′(τ) = 0 with analytic C′, C″; steps clamped to one coarse step; C″ < 0 required; stop at 10⁻¹⁴ turns.
  4. b = C(τ̂)/Σ|Sk|²; a = (P0 − b·S0)/N; τ wrapped to [−½, ½).
Why FFTFIT

It is the standard pulsar-timing method: continuous shifts, fast, and the maximum-likelihood estimator for white noise (the weak-signal regime). The SNR sweep showed it within 1 % of the theoretical optimum up to −5 dB; it only loses at very high SNR where self-noise dominates (1.5× at +10 dB, 2.4× at +20 dB), irrelevant for the small-antenna target.

3. The uncertainty

\(\hat\tau\) is where \(C'(\tau) = 0\), and \(C'\) is a linear combination of the data: \(C' = \sum_j d_j\, p_j\), with \(d\) from the template slope (largest on the pulse flanks). Noise moves \(C'\) by \(\delta C'\); the peak moves until \(C' = 0\) again: \(\delta\tau = -\delta C'/C''\). Therefore

\[ \operatorname{var}(\hat\tau) = \frac{d^{\mathsf T}\, \mathrm{Cov}\; d}{C''^{\,2}} \qquad \text{(and similarly } \sigma_b \text{ from } C(\tau)\text{)} \]
\(\hat\tau\)
the fitted shift [turns]
\(p_j\)
profile value in phase bin \(j\)
\(d\)
the weights that give \(C'(\hat\tau) = \sum_j d_j p_j\); they follow the slope of the shifted template (the DC and Nyquist harmonics left out)
\(\mathrm{Cov}\)
noise covariance matrix of the profile bins (below)
\(C'\), \(C''\)
first and second derivative of \(C(\tau)\) with respect to \(\tau\), at the peak
\(\sigma_b\)
error of the amplitude \(b\)

This is first-order error propagation (the delta method). For white noise it equals the least-squares/Fisher error. For a Gaussian pulse it gives σTOA ≈ √2·σt/SNR.

The noise covariance Cov

There are two noise models.

  • 'radiometer' (default). Each time bin has variance m²/(Bnoise·Δt), where m = a + b·s is the fitted model. So the pulsar's own contribution, self-noise, is included. Each phase bin then has variance σtb²·weight2/weight², and its covariance with the neighbours comes from weightX.
  • 'offpulse'. The variance and the lag covariances are measured from the off-pulse residuals.
Why the fitted model, and which bandwidth

The ground-truth rule: the observer does not know the true noise level. So σ comes from the fitted baseline and amplitude.

For the bandwidth, main.m passes Bnoise = the noise bandwidth of the observer's own dedispersion taper: 391.6 MHz, or 3.0596 MHz per channel. It does not use the value for both simulated tapers together (389.6 MHz), because only the simulator knows that one. Receiver noise passes only the observer's taper, so the value is exact for it. Where the pulsar dominates (high SNR, on the pulse), the true value is about 0.5 % lower.

What the error bar does not know

It measures the sharpness of the correlation peak. It assumes you are on the right peak (at low SNR a noise peak can win: outliers). It also assumes that the template is right; otherwise χ² catches it. That is why there are detection and the χ² flag. In an early radar test (full band, no excision, 5 Oct 2026) the fit locked onto radar spikes: the stated σ was about 1 µs, the actual errors about 2000 µs rms.

4. The χ² quality flag

\[ \chi^2_\mathrm{red} = \frac{1}{n - 3} \sum_{j\,\in\,\text{on-pulse}} \frac{(p_j - m_j)^2}{v_j} \]
\(p_j\)
profile value in phase bin \(j\)
\(m_j\)
the fitted model \(a + b\,s(\phi_j - \hat\tau)\) in that bin
\(v_j\)
its predicted noise variance (from the noise model)
\(n\)
number of on-pulse bins; 3 = the fitted parameters \(a, b, \tau\)

The sum runs over the on-pulse bins, where the model is above 1 % of the peak (OnPulseFrac). It is the average squared residual, in units of the predicted noise. If the profile is template + noise with the predicted variance, χ²red ≈ 1 ± √(2/n): about ± 0.09 for ~260 bins. A sub-int is flagged (flagChi2) when χ²red > MaxRedChi2 = 2.

Why threshold 2

It is far above the clean scatter, and above the known excess at high SNR (1.05–1.1). It is far below what a radar does (939–1625). And it deliberately does not react to moderate RFI: a carrier gives χ² ~1.3, and its TOAs are still valid.

Noise-model check: pChi2

The problem. The threshold 2 was set on the full band, with about 260 on-pulse bins. The channel path fits about 263 × 128 ≈ 34,000 bins at once. There the clean scatter of χ²red is only ± 0.008, so a threshold of 2 is about 130σ away. The flag still catches gross failures. But error bars a few percent too small now pass silently. An example: with the narrow carriers of the realistic RFI scenario, χ² was 1.22 (28σ), and the error bars were 3.4 % too small.

What the code does. pChi2 reports such cases, without changing which TOAs count as good.

  • toa.pChi2 (and toa.total.pChi2) is the probability of a χ² at least this large, if the profile is template + noise with the modelled variances. For clean data it is spread evenly over [0, 1]. It is near 0 when the residuals are larger than the noise model allows.
  • Neighbouring phase bins are correlated (linear assignment, ρ ≈ ¼), so χ² is not an ordinary χ². Its variance is 2 Σij ρij² instead of 2n. The fit already has the lag covariances, which give this exactly. χ² is then matched to g·χ²ν with the same mean and variance (g = var/(2 dof), ν = 2 dof²/var), and the tail is taken with gammainc. With uncorrelated bins this is the ordinary χ² tail.
  • The summary counts the sub-ints with pChi2 < Chi2Alarm (default 10⁻³). This is a report only: flagChi2 and MaxRedChi2 = 2 still decide which TOAs are good.
Why report, not reject

Three options were weighed: keep the threshold 2 alone, add a probability threshold that drops TOAs, or report pChi2 without dropping anything. A sensitive threshold that drops TOAs would throw away every TOA in the narrow-carrier case, whose error bars are only 3.4 % too small. For navigation that is worse than the problem.

So gross failures, such as a wrong peak, are still dropped by the threshold 2. A noise model that is a few percent off is made visible. The fix for such a case is the per-channel noise calibration (the switch noiseCal, off by default).

Test: testOptimalTOA test 4 draws 800 profiles per SNR level with the exact covariance of a blanked fold, for 'equal' and 'optimal' weighting. pChi2 is uniform by a Kolmogorov–Smirnov test at the 10⁻³ level.

History

5. Combining channels: equal weights

For a fold with channels, 'Weighting','equal' (the function's default) sums the channel profiles: p = Σc profc. The shared helper combineChannels does this:

  • Exclusion rule: a channel is used only if it has data (W > 0) in every phase bin where any channel has data. A channel missing in some bins would leave a dip in the sum and pull the TOA. Partial weights stay in.
  • Noise: channels independent → var(pj) = Σc scj²·W2cj/Wcj², covariances for all lags d, with scj = mc(φj)/√(Bc·binDt) and each channel's own fitted level mc.
  • Output toa.nChanUsed. Single-channel folds give bit-identical results to before.
Limits of equal weights

Equal weights are not optimal when channels differ in noise (heavily blanked channels, carrier channels), and the exclusion rule can drop whole channels. In one test, 1-turn sub-ints with 100 µs blanks removed channels 110–115 everywhere: more data was lost than blanked. That is why optimal weighting exists (next section).

6. Optimal weighting — the default in main.m

'Weighting','optimal' fits all channels at once:

\[ \mathrm{prof}_{cj} = a_c + b\, s_c\, T(\phi_j - \tau) + \text{noise} \]
\(\mathrm{prof}_{cj}\)
folded profile of channel \(c\) in phase bin \(j\)
\(a_c\)
a baseline per channel
\(b\)
one pulse amplitude for all channels
\(s_c\)
the known relative gain of channel \(c\) ('ChannelGain', default 1: observer knowledge of the pulsar spectrum × bandpass)
\(T\)
the template (peak 1 at phase 0); here \(T\) is not the period
\(\phi_j\)
phase of bin \(j\) [turns]
\(\tau\)
one common shift [turns]
Why one common amplitude, not one per channel

At −54 dB each channel's pulse has SNR ≪ 1 per sub-int; 128 free amplitudes would fit noise. One amplitude with a known spectral shape uses all channels' information for the one thing that matters, the shift.

  • Each bin and channel is weighted by 1/variance, from the radiometer model with the current fit, or from per-channel off-pulse residuals. The fit is iterated until it converges (|Δτ| < 10⁻¹²), at most MaxIterations = 10 times. Whether it converged is kept in toa.converged. A sub-int that did not converge keeps its TOA, and the summary counts it.
  • For fixed τ, ac and b by weighted least squares; τ maximizes N(τ)/√Dn(τ), the weighted matched filter: FFT correlations on the upsampled grid, a bounded search (fminbnd), then Newton on the analytic slope (a value-only search stops at ~10⁻¹⁰ turns).
  • Error bars: sandwich \(\mathrm{Cov} = A^{-1}BA^{-1}\), \(A = J^\mathsf{T}WJ\), \(B = J^\mathsf{T}W\Sigma WJ\): \(J\) the derivatives of the model with respect to the fit parameters, \(W\) the fit weights (diagonal), \(\Sigma\) the full noise covariance of every channel (all lags). These \(A\), \(B\), \(W\) are matrices, not amplitude, bandwidth or taper. The weights ignore neighbour covariances (correlation ~0.26, small loss); the error bars do not.
  • Empty bins weigh nothing, so there is no exclusion rule and no dips; nChanUsed = channels with data; new output chanBaseline. amp = b·Σsc (the summed pulse), baseline = Σac.
Why the sandwich form

The weights are an approximation of the inverse covariance (they ignore neighbour correlations). The sandwich estimator is honest even when the fit's weights are not exactly the inverse covariance; the plain A⁻¹ would only be right if they were.

A side benefit, seen in the RFI tests: weighting by 1/level² is a built-in notch for narrowband RFI. A carrier that cut the full-band SNR to 74 leaves the channel path at 92.

History

Equal weights: step A2a, 7 Oct 2026. Optimal weighting: step A2c-1, the same day. toa.converged is kept since 14 Oct 2026; before, the flag was dropped.

7. The template

main.m uses gaussianTemplate(nBin, ephem.profileFWHM): a periodic Gaussian, peak 1 at bin 1 (images at −2…+2 turns summed). estimateTOA accepts any [NBin × 1] profile with phase 0 at bin 1, so real pulsars need only a different template source (a multi-component model or a high-SNR observed profile). Its phase 0 then defines the TOA.

gaussianTemplate and foldToTOA also take a component matrix, with rows [amplitude, centre, FWHM] in turns. The template is then the sum of the Gaussians, scaled to a maximum of 1. Phase 0 is the components' reference point, which is not necessarily the peak.

An example: B0329+54 at 1410 MHz has five components (arXiv:1703.03582, Table 2). There is a core at 0, a broad component at −0.003 turns, and outer ones at −0.032, +0.021 and +0.026 turns. A single Gaussian with the catalogue W50 would put only half its flux in the pulse area, and so overstate the peak by 3 dB.

Bins must be fine compared with the template

The problem. A profile bin is the pulse averaged over a time bin (binDt·f₀ turns) and spread over about one phase bin. The template, however, is the unsmoothed pulse at the bin centres. If the bins are coarse compared with the pulse, the model no longer matches the data, and the error bars come out too small.

What the code does. estimateTOA warns (estimateTOA:resolution) when the bin resolution r = max(binDt·f₀, 1/NBin) is more than 1/10 of the template width w. It returns both, in info.binResolution and info.templateWidth.

The template width. w comes from the template's harmonics, over the harmonics that the fit uses:

\[ w = \frac{\sqrt{3\ln 2}}{\pi\,k_t} \approx \frac{0.459}{k_t}, \qquad k_t^2 = \frac{\sum_k k^4 |S_k|^2}{\sum_k k^2 |S_k|^2} \]

The reason: averaging over r turns multiplies harmonic k by sin(πkr)/(πkr) ≈ 1 − (πkr)²/6. The fit weights harmonic k by k²|Sk|². So the model error grows as (πr kt)²/6. For a Gaussian, w is exactly its FWHM. So the measurements below, made with the Gaussian pulse before this definition, hold unchanged.

What coarse bins cost. Measured at −5 dB, as the rms error divided by the stated error bar (1 is honest):

Binsrms error / error bar
fine1.04
time bins of w/2.52.04
time bins of w/51.08
phase bins alone of w/1.61.16
phase bins alone of w/2.4–6.41.05

Coarse time bins are the critical side. Main's settings give r = w/102, far inside the limit.

Why 1/10. At low SNR the cost of coarse bins is larger. B0329+54's five-component template at SNR 7 gave error bars 18 % too small with bins of 1/5.6 of its narrowest component, and 8 % too small with 1/11.

Why the spectral width. A simpler width, the narrowest region above half the template's maximum, misses narrow features below half maximum, such as B0329+54's core tip and outer components. For B0329+54 it gives 0.0104 turns, so even NBin 1024 would pass. The spectral width follows the features that carry the timing: 0.0077 turns for B0329+54, whose narrowest component is 0.0055. So NBin 1024 warns (1/7.9) and 2048 passes (1/15.8), as the pulls showed.

Caveat. A noisy template, for example one measured from data, has a white floor of harmonics. That makes w far too small: 1 % noise on a 0.05-turn Gaussian at NBin 1024 gives 0.0012. Smooth such a template, or limit the fit with MaxHarmonic.

History

The resolution warning exists since 9 Oct 2026, with a limit of 1/5. On 20 Oct the limit was tightened to 1/10, and w changed from the half-maximum width to the spectral width. Multi-component templates also came on 20 Oct.

9. Validation

  • Noise-free (1 s, 99 TOAs): median σ 0.466 µs vs 0.469 µs from a NumPy Monte Carlo; rms/pred 1.053; χ² 109.9/99.
  • −5 dB Monte Carlo (10 noise seeds, 90 TOAs): rms/pred 0.950 ± 0.075, χ²red 0.91, 72 % within 1σ; σ only 0.5 % above the exact optimum.
  • SNR sweep −25…+20 dB: error bars honest for SNR per sub-int ≳ 5 (good TOAs); outliers below that, removed by detection.
  • tests/testChannelTOA.m (equal weights): the full band is bit-identical; the error bars equal the explicit full-covariance formula to 4·10⁻¹⁶; the channel fold agrees with the summed fold.
  • tests/testOptimalTOA.m (optimal weighting): it equals FFTFIT for one channel, to 1.7·10⁻¹⁶ turns; the sandwich error bars equal an explicit computation; a Monte Carlo with the exact covariance is unbiased, with pulls of standard deviation 1.006–1.037, and a scatter 0.955–0.963 of equal weighting's.
