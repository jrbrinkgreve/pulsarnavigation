1. Why blank, and why here

Blank, do not subtract

At −5 dB one radar pulse carries about the energy of one pulsar pulse; at −54 dB it carries the energy of ~80,000. Any subtraction leaves a residual proportional to the RFI; even 99.9 % suppression would leave more radar than pulsar. Zeroed samples carry no RFI, no noise and no pulsar: weight 0, nothing left over. Zeroing (rather than deleting) keeps the regular time grid that coherent dedispersion needs.

Before dedispersion, per channel

In a 3.125 MHz channel before dedispersion, a 2 µs radar pulse is ~8 samples in about 2 channels. After dedispersion it is spread over thousands of samples and mixed with good data. So excision works on the raw channel voltages, and the cost of a blank is tiny.

Excision must be honest about what it removed

Dedispersion spreads every blank over the filter's reach (~130–310 output samples per channel). Each output sample then contains only a fraction of valid data. If the fold ignored that, it would see a dip wherever blanking happened, and blanking that correlates with pulse phase (a radar locked to the pulsar, or blanks triggered by the pulse itself) would bias the TOA. blankingWeights computes the valid fraction and the exact noise of every detected bin, and the fold uses them.

2 · detectRFI validated (B2)

[mask, info] = detectRFI(info_chan, ...)

Full interface and help text of detectRFI →

Purpose

Flag samples in the raw channel files whose power is too high to be noise or pulsar, using only the channel data and the channelizer settings (ground-truth rule). Output: a mask of rows [channel, first, last] (1-based channel samples).

The statistic

Per channel, power p = |x|² of the raw channel samples, averaged over windows of n = 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 samples (stride max(1, n/2)). A window is flagged if its mean exceeds ηn·m, with m the channel's baseline. Several window lengths catch both short strong bursts and longer weaker ones.

Exact thresholds

For Gaussian noise the window sum is not a simple Gamma variable, because neighbouring channel samples are slightly correlated (ρ(1) = 0.05, from the oversampled channelizer). Exactly:

Σn p / m = Σk λk·Ek,   Ek unit exponentials,   λ = eig(toeplitz(ρ(0..n−1)))

with ρ(d) = Σh(r)h(r + dD)/Σh² from the channelizer prototype. Its survival function is a chain of exponential stages (a phase-type distribution), [1 0 … 0]·expm(Qx)·1, computed exactly without cancellation. ηn solves it = PFA (default 10⁻⁶): 13.82, 8.39, 5.39, 3.68, 2.69 for n = 1…16.

Why exact, not a matched Gamma

The first version used a Gamma distribution with the right variance. Its tail is thinner than the true one, which gave 1.02 / 1.04 / 1.08–1.10 × the intended false flags at 10⁻³ / 10⁻⁴ / 10⁻⁶: visible in the first test run. The exact form moves the thresholds by less than 0.5 % but makes the false-flag rate what it says.

Baseline

m per channel and 16 ms block (BaselineTime) = median(p)/ln 2 over fully supported samples: exact for exponential power (a Gaussian voltage's power), and RFI in a fraction f of the samples raises it by only ~1.4f. A second pass (Passes = 2) recomputes it without the flagged samples.

Constant-envelope channels flag less, on purpose

In a channel dominated by a carrier or a GNSS signal the power is not exponential and the median-based baseline comes out up to 1/ln 2 too high, so those channels flag less. That is intended: such RFI is not excised; the optimal channel weighting gives those channels little weight instead (see results).

Mask

Flagged windows are widened by Guard samples (default = the prototype's half-length in channel samples, (3853 − 1)/2/192 → 10) and merged.

Why windows only up to 16 samples (decision 8 Oct)

The pulsar itself raises the power on-pulse. At −5 dB by 32 %: 256-sample windows blanked 14 % of the on-pulse data. With windows ≤ 16 samples (3.8 µs), from −20 dB down no window is triggered by the pulsar (probability ≤ 2.3·10⁻⁶). The target is weak signal, where this is safe.

Frequency guard (B5b-2, 8 Oct)

A strong event (peak sample power ≥ FreqGuardMin = 100 × baseline, i.e. +20 dB) is also blanked, on the same samples, in FreqGuard channels on each side (default 7, Jasper's choice from the locked-radar sweep). Weak flags (noise, the pulsar) never reach 100× and do not spread.

Why

A strong radar's spectral sidelobes in nearby channels are below the noise per sample, so the power test cannot see them. If the radar is locked to the pulsar's period, those sidelobes add up coherently in the fold and bias the TOA. Blanking the same samples a few channels further costs ~0.004 % of the data per guard channel for this radar. See locked radar.

3 · blankChannels validated (B1)

info_chanB = blankChannels(info_chan, mask, outBase, ...)

Full interface and help text of blankChannels →

Writes blanked copies <outBase>_ch###.dat of the channel files: masked samples exactly 0, all other samples bit-exact, streamed in blocks (result independent of BlockSize). It never overwrites the raw files. The mask may be unsorted, overlapping or partly outside the file: it is clipped and normalized per channel (sorted, merged where overlapping or touching).

The output is info_chan with new file / chanFiles, plus rawFile, mask (normalized), nMaskRows, blankedSamples, blankedFraction, blockSize. dedisperseChannels and blankingWeights take it unchanged.

Why separate files

The unblanked channel files stay for with/without comparisons. main.m writes the excision chain to its own file names (…_blanked, test_dedispB_*). (The unit tests use their own frozen copies in data/test/chan/ since 13 Oct 2026.)

Tests (tests/testBlankChannels.m): 1727 mask rows (5.2 % blanked) exact; merge/clip cases; block size 4099 = one block; empty mask → byte-identical copies; dedisperseChannels on the result: 83 unblanked channels bit-identical to the unblanked run, a blanked channel changes only within the filter reach.

4 · blankingWeights validated (A3a)

info_w = blankingWeights(info_chan, info_dc, info_det, mask, outFile, ...)

Full interface and help text of blankingWeights →

The physics: blanking before a filter

Dedispersion is a filter h spanning hundreds of channel samples. An output sample y(a) = Σh(l)·x(a−l) mixes blanked and valid inputs. For Gaussian input, the covariance of two output samples is

C(a, a+τ) = Σl h(l)·h*(l+τ)·keep(a−l)

a convolution of the keep-mask with qτ = h·h*(·+τ): one FFT per sample lag τ = 0…(Lmax+1)n−1 (32 lags for Lmax 7, n 4). From it, for every detected bin k and channel:

  • W = valid fraction = mean of C(a,a) over the bin's samples / unblanked value;
  • V and X(L) = (1/n²)·Σ|C|² over the sample pairs of bins k and k+L, relative to the radiometer value (Isserlis again).

Long time bins (fixed 12 Oct): when the lag range reaches the filter length, qτ at the last lag is a single number; the FFT is now taken along dimension 1, so it stays a column (before: an M×M array and a crash). Since 12 Oct each lag is also added into V, X as soon as it is computed, so only one lag is in memory (the weight files are bit-identical to before). Still limited: run time grows with the number of lags, and each channel is processed whole: the weight array is 4.8 GB at L = 1 s, above the 4 GB default MaxMemoryGB (see limits; block processing designed, not built).

Why exact streams, not a simple rule g(W)

The plan was a variance rule between W² and W. Experiment 3a (tests/expBlankingVariance.m) showed neither holds: errors up to ±45 %. Physics: in a dispersed channel, lag maps to frequency (it is a chirp), so a blank removes a frequency slice from the affected outputs (fewer independent frequencies, towards W), while within a short bin the samples are partly correlated (towards W²). Short blanks spread thin only scale the amplitude (W²). No single rule covers all masks, so every bin carries its exact W, V, X(L).

Code

  • Mask rows [channel, first, last], clipped, overlaps allowed.
  • h per channel: an impulse through applyInverseDispersion with that channel's settings and FFT size (checked: same nPast/nFuture as dedisperseChannels).
  • Unblanked channels: W = 1 and the unblanked V0, X0 from their own h (equal to the spectrum constants of powerCovariance to 5·10⁻⁹).
  • MinWeight (10⁻⁶): W below it → W = V = X = 0.
  • Weight file: float32 [nChan × (2+Lmax) × N] (per bin: W of all channels, then V, then X(1)…X(Lmax)), same time grid as the power file. ~0.3 s per blanked channel + 2 s for the 128 filters.

Mean with the true channel spectrum (7 Oct, Jasper's decision)

Channel samples are not white: the channelizer's output is flat in the useful band but rolls off to 0.5 at the channel Nyquist (mean 0.951), with an autocorrelation significant to lag ~19. Blanking mixes the roll-off region into the band, so W uses the true autocorrelation Rx (from the channelizer prototype, one extra FFT of the mask product per lag). Effect: ~1 % for nearly empty bins, ≤ 0.07 % for W ≥ 0.1. V and X keep the white form (error bars only; within 1–2 % in heavily blanked bins). Option InputSpectrum = 'channelizer' (default) or 'white'.

Why the mean must be this exact

At −54 dB the pulse peak is ~4·10⁻⁶ of the baseline. Any error in W that depends on pulse phase must be far below that. Phase-uniform errors only shift the baseline (harmless).

A fold rule found with it

A detected bin with W = 0 must not add its power either. Leftover power in such bins (effective W ~4·10⁻⁷, from filter tails) biased nearly empty phase bins by up to +285 %. Now foldProfile drops it (see fold).

Tests (tests/testBlankingWeights.m)

  1. Exactness: W, V, X(1..7) vs the direct covariance matrix H·diag(keep)·H′ for 221 bins in channels 1, 64, 128 (blanks 1–3001 samples): max difference 3·10⁻⁸.
  2. Consistency: empty mask = the NoiseCoeffs fold; far from blanks = unblanked constants; messy mask = clean mask; channel autocorrelation from the prototype = measured (max difference 7·10⁻⁴, ≤ 2.3σ).
  3. End to end, voltages blanked before dedispersion with a zoo of masks (random 20 µs, broadband impulses, radar at 373 Hz and a phase-locked 400 Hz, 30 µs on the pulse in channels 90–100, 100 µs every 1.1 ms in 110–115, a 30 ms gap, a dead channel): detected power E[P] = W·E[Punblanked] to −0.2·10⁻⁵ ± 1.4·10⁻⁵; folded mean unbiased off-pulse and on the pulse in pulse-blanked channels (−0.0009 ± 0.0014 of the pulse height; a naive fold is off by −0.221); noise within ≤ 1.6σ per valid-fraction group; TOAs consistent.

What this shows, and why it leads to a periodic mask

At −5 dB the sidelobe radar is harmless with or without excision. But at −35 dB a 2 µs pulse is only ~2× the noise per sample in its channels, so most pulses are missed one by one. Over a scan the sidelobes then dominate what a rotating radar leaves: the main beam (excised, ~0 left with the frequency guard) carries ~41 ms × full power per 10 s scan, the −35 dB sidelobes ~3 ms × full power, of which ~60 % is missed. If locked to the pulsar, that is ~1.8·10⁻³ of the baseline in the fold, ≈ 450× the −54 dB pulsar.

Per-pulse thresholds cannot fix this. But the main-beam passage (~15–40 pulses at +38 dB) measures the radar's period, pulse phase and channels precisely, so its weak sidelobe pulses can be blanked where they are predicted to be. That is B6. Unlocked, the leftover is only phase-uniform noise.

The periodic mask that does this is described in section 8; with it, the −35 dB sidelobe radar is blanked completely.

8 · periodicRFI: blanking predicted pulses (B6) validated (B6)

[rows, info] = periodicRFI(info_chan, info_rfi, ...)

Full interface and help text of periodicRFI → · in main.m with excision and periodicMask (default true until 9 Oct, false since 9 Oct: see the limits below): its rows are added to detectRFI's mask before blankChannels.

Why predict instead of detect

What makes periodic RFI dangerous — regular timing, so it can pile up at fixed pulse phases (section 6) — also makes it predictable: once the period and the time of one pulse are known, every pulse is known, also those too weak to be seen (the −35 dB sidelobes of section 7). RFI with irregular timing (Poisson impulses, PRF jitter) cannot be predicted, but it cannot fold coherently either; it is only extra noise. So prediction covers exactly the dangerous case.

Method

  1. Pulses. detectRFI lists every flagged interval (info.events: channel, interval, peak). Intervals at the same time (within 8 samples) in neighbouring channels are one pulse; its time is the centre of the interval in its strongest channel.
  2. Emitters. Narrowband pulses whose strongest channels are within 2 of each other form one candidate emitter. Broadband pulses (≥ 32 channels, impulses) form a group of their own, because their strongest channel is random.
  3. Period. Candidate periods are the gaps between a pulse and its next three, divided by 1–8 (missed pulses leave gaps of 2P, 3P, …). For each candidate: the best phase, and the m of n pulses within ±4 samples (1 µs) of t0 + kP. How likely is that for random times? One pulse sets the phase, each other fits by chance with q = 9/P:
    p = n · P( Binomial(n − 1, q) ≥ m − 1 ), accepted if p × (number of candidates) < 10⁻⁶ and m ≥ 6
    The least likely (most significant) candidate wins, which also picks P over P/2 (same pulses, twice the chance). t0 and P are then refined by least squares.
  4. Mask. Every predicted pulse in the file is blanked unconditionally, over its width + 2 × (time guard + tolerance + 4 × the prediction uncertainty), in the emitter's channels ± the frequency guard.
Two design choices
  • Unconditional blanking. The mask then does not depend on the noise values, so blankingWeights stays exact and nothing is truncated. Cost: ~0.05–0.13 % of the data for one radar.
  • The significance test. The first version accepted an emitter when 6 pulses fitted; random impulses then produced 3 fake emitters, because for short trial periods 6 random pulses fit somewhere. The binomial chance, corrected for the number of candidates tried, fixed this.
The pulsar is not masked

The pulsar is periodic too, but it is dispersed: in the raw channels its pulse arrives 59 µs later per channel (at 1.3 GHz), so neighbouring channels do not combine into one simultaneous pulse, and its detections jitter across the 0.5 ms pulse, far outside ±1 µs. main.m at −5 dB without RFI: 245 detected pulses, 0 emitters, identical TOAs.

Limits of version 1
  • Fixed PRF only. A staggered PRF (a repeating pattern of intervals, common for ATC radars) fits no single period; such a group is reported (info.unfitted), and its detected pulses are still blanked by detectRFI.
  • Needs ≥ 6 detected pulses of an emitter in the same data.
  • Built for short files: it compares pulse pairs and writes one mask row per pulse and channel.

9. Limits

Known limits of excision
  • Data-dependent: blanking removes the highest samples, so the kept noise is slightly truncated. Negligible at weak SNR; at −5 dB it costs 0.4 % of pulse amplitude.
  • Locked RFI: with the frequency guard and the periodic mask, 0.41 µs bias at −54 dB for an exactly locked radar on the worst phase (guard alone: 2 µs). The guard width is fixed, not scaled with the event's strength. The periodic mask handles fixed PRFs only (section 8).
  • Carrier / GNSS channels have baselines 7–47× the noise, so impulses there are not caught (those channels carry little weight).
  • Window length ≤ 16 samples: weak bursts longer than 3.8 µs are found only above the 16-sample threshold (2.69×). Weak repeated pulses (rotating-radar sidelobes at −35 dB) are mostly missed one by one; the periodic mask (section 8) blanks them by prediction. No spectral kurtosis or whole-channel flags yet.
  • Flat spectrum within a channel assumed. A channel only partly covered by strong wideband RFI (the band edges of an LTE carrier) has a lopsided noise spectrum: detectRFI over-flags there (540 vs ~3 windows), and the detected-power noise exceeds the fold model (red. χ² 1.04 at LTE 0 dB). Measured in the realistic scenario (B5e, 9 Oct): TOA error bars 0.39 % too small, 3.4 % with narrow Inmarsat-like carriers (results); a per-channel noise measured on the data is planned.
  • Periodic mask with dense detections (found 9 Oct, B5e): hundreds of false flags per channel (lopsided channels) let periodicRFI accept false emitters (~14.1 kHz) and blank ~30 % of the data. Therefore off by default since 9 Oct; fix designed, not implemented (limitations, 2026-10-09_handover-B-closed.md).
  • Noise-like RFI (LTE, OFDM) is undetectable by any power or kurtosis test; it is extra noise in its channels, handled by the optimal weighting.
  • The 0.1 µs rise time is an assumption, to be checked against ITU masks.
