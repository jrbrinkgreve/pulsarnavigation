Development history

How the pipeline got to its current form, session by session, and every design decision with the reason it was taken. The dated logs in logs/ have the full detail.

In short

The full-band chain was built and validated first (block 1, to 6 Oct). The reference scenario then showed that the target is ~54 dB below the noise and full of interference. So a channelized front end (A) and RFI excision (B) came next, before the fast simulator.

The review week (12–16 Oct) went through every stage again, to understand and refine it. Then the processing became one function, cut at the IQ file (19 Oct), and block C, the fast simulator, started (20 Oct). It made the first TOA budgets for the real scenario: a ship near the Netherlands timing B0329+54, and J0437 from further south. Every design decision is listed below, with its reason.

1. Session by session

29–30 September — the noise-free pipeline

All stages built: generator, dispersion, IQ conversion, coherent dedispersion, power detection, folding, TOA fit (FFTFIT). The old dispersion kernel's sign error, an fftshift offset, the Hann-window attenuation, out-of-band wrap-around, the unremoved IQ filter delay and the halved IQ amplitude were found and fixed (bugs #1–7); the missing fold covariance (weightX, #8) too. Project notes started.

1 October — noise in, first validation

  • Receiver stage (addNoiseAndRFI, rfiSource, expectedPowerModel) added to the repo; notes synced to the code.
  • Parameters changed by Jasper: fLO 1.3 GHz, fs 800 MHz, −5 dB, 1 turn per sub-int, 2048 bins.
  • Refactor: pipelineParams.m, helpers moved to functions/ (gaussianTemplate, noiseBandwidth, loadInfo); runMonteCarlo.m.
  • Reference run at −5 dB (seed 43): SNR 93.4, σ 2.880 µs. Monte Carlo over 10 seeds: error bars honest (ratio 0.950).
  • Jasper flagged bandpass filtering / noise bandwidth as not yet fully understood → review item.

2 October — review only

Walkthrough of the mathematics behind each stage (generator, chirp, SNR definition and Fisher information, complex envelope, radiometer equation via Isserlis and Parseval, fold bookkeeping, FFTFIT errors). No code changes.

5 October — how realistic is it?

  • Physical fidelity: exact for what it models over 0.1–1 s; simplified spectrum, profile and receiver; −5 dB is a big dish; missing Doppler, DM variations, clock and scintillation (limitations).
  • Bandpass review closed: two bandwidths (∫W² for the mean, (∫W²)²/∫W⁴ for the variance), all effects ≤ 1.5 %; the exact optimum added to expectedPowerModel.
  • Phase D SNR sweep (runSNRSweep.m): honest error bars above SNR ≈ 10, outliers below; FFTFIT optimal at weak signal.
  • RFI test: carriers and GNSS cost SNR, impulses harmless, the radar destroys TOAs with confident error bars. GNSS crash fixed (#10).
  • Phase E started: detectPulsar, the χ² flag, good TOAs in main.m.
  • Jasper: design for the weak-signal regime (small-antenna hardware); −50 dB is an operating point, not H0; target DM up to ~60 (later 100).

6 October — block 1 closed, scenario, new plan, front end started

  • Morning: phase E sweep (good TOAs outlier-free), long H0 run (noise model complete, false alarms as designed), threshold sweep (SNR ≥ 6–7), centroid offset closed as a fluctuation → Block 1 closed.
  • Afternoon — reference scenario: Jasper's 3×3 array; worst case −54 dB; ATNF table; J0437 ~20 h per TOA; normal pulsars needed; DM target 100; sky temperature in Tsys.
  • Evening — plan changed: excision before the fast simulator, with a channelized front end. Built and validated: channelizeIQ, dedisperseChannels (+ AllowRefOutsideBand), experiment 3a, detectChannels + powerCovariance. Tests became functions (#11). Labels A–E introduced.

7 October — block A, the fold and the fit

  • A1a (NoiseCoeffs), A1b (DataWeights), A2a / A2b (channels in TOA fit and detection, combineChannels).
  • A3a blankingWeights, with two findings: a W = 0 bin must not add power; the mean needs the true channel spectrum.
  • A2c-1: optimal weighting in estimateTOA.
  • Q&A on the walkthrough (logs/2026-10-07_qa.md).

8 October — block A complete, block B

  • A2c-2 (optimal detection), A4 (switch in main.m, channel path default) → block A complete.
  • B1 blankChannels, B2 detectRFI, B3 excision in main.m (default on), B4 runRFITest.
  • B5a radar rise time (realistic default), B5b locked radar → B5b-2 frequency guard (default 7), B5c rotating antenna. Ionosphere noted as an open item.
  • B6 periodic mask (periodicRFI, default on): a radar locked to the pulsar and seen only through −35 dB sidelobes now leaves 1.2·10⁻¹⁰ of the baseline. B5d LTE-like noise source. Documentation site created.

9 October — B5e, the realistic scenario

  • Decision "measure first" (Jasper): B5e, a realistic L-band RFI environment for the reference hardware (rfiList / rfiRealistic), with a per-channel noise check in runRFITest.
  • Lopsided channels understood: a flat-channel formula predicted the affected channels and the effect on the TOA error bars (0.39 %); worst case narrow noise-like carriers, 3.4 %. Found: the periodic mask accepts false emitters when detections are dense.
  • Block B closed for now: the periodic mask off by default; the mask fix and the per-channel noise calibration parked with designs (2026-10-09_handover-B-closed.md).
  • A question from a meeting, "does a higher f_out improve detection?", answered with an experiment: no, once the bins resolve the pulse; the information is set by B·T (expSamplingRate, tests).
  • Jasper's idea to fold the channels without dedispersing them investigated: possible, with a per-channel delay and a smeared template; noted, not implemented.

12–13 October — the review week

  • Goal (Jasper): understand and refine the whole pipeline, one stage at a time, before block C. Refinements found along the way are noted, not implemented; minor and simple changes are made and validated one by one (13 Oct).
  • 12 Oct: project review finished; blankingWeights crash with long time bins and its lag memory fixed; B5e and B5b-2 validated by reruns; stage 1 (the generator) reviewed.
  • 13 Oct: code review of the whole codebase (33 items): minor fixes; main.m checks reused processing and fold files; runAllTests; a generator unit test; the tests get their own frozen data. Then a full read-through and a deeper analysis (docs errors fixed; J0437's binary orbit, the position prior and visibility noted). Stages 2–3 reviewed. These docs rewritten to explain why each algorithm is built as it is, with computed figures.

14–16 October — the review week, continued

  • Stages 4–11 and the drivers walked through, one at a time: the IQ stage, the channelizer, RFI detection, the periodic mask, blanking, per-channel dedispersion, detection, the data weights, the fold, the TOA fit and detection, and main.m with the run scripts. Refinements were noted, not implemented (notes §10 item 11, the roadmap); small text errors were fixed.
  • 14 Oct, small fixes (Jasper: "fix the small things autonomously"): estimateTOA keeps its convergence flag (toa.converged), and main.m warns when excision meets a bright pulsar (main:brightExcision). Validated by Jasper's runAllTests on 15 Oct.
  • 14 Oct, docs: every remaining page in the 13 Oct format, with computed figures (Jasper: "continue with all the other pages in that format").
  • 16 Oct, the χ² threshold: on the channel path a threshold of 2 is about 130σ away, so noise-model errors pass silently. Jasper chose to keep 2 as the drop rule and add a report, pChi2 (TOA). Validated by Jasper's runs.
  • 16 Oct, one processing function: the first proposal, one function from the sky to the TOAs, was rejected by Jasper. Real IQ data enter between the simulation and the processing, and the operational processing will run on an FPGA or GPU. The revised proposal cuts the code at the IQ file.
  • 16 Oct, docs: all equations in LaTeX, each with a list that defines its symbols (Jasper: "equations look cluttered").

19 October — the processing as one function

  • Jasper's choice: "start at IQ for the boundary, and include both front ends".
  • processIQ (IQ file in, fold out) and foldToTOA (fold in, TOAs and detection out). main.m on them gives identical outputs (validated; Jasper: "this one time consider it validated by me").
  • The run scripts moved onto them; the full band and the RFI cases are bit-identical.
  • A3b: 50 seeds on the default channel path give a ratio of 0.956 ± 0.034. The error bars are honest end to end over seeds, for the first time. A pChi2 excess at −5 dB was traced to the two band-edge channels (roadmap item 3); it is gone at −15 dB. runH0 on the channel path: false alarms at the design rate.
  • Item 3 and the per-channel noise calibration stay parked (Jasper).

20 October — block C, the fast simulator

  • Jasper: "run the sweep in the background and start C". The weak-regime sweep on the channel path confirms the design rule of SNR ≥ 6–7 per TOA.
  • simulateFold draws folded profiles from their statistics: one channel first (C1), validated against the voltage chain (C2), then channels (C4a), RFI from a measured profile (C4b; Jasper: option a), observing passes (C3a), scintillation (C3b) and adaptive sub-ints (C3c). Then dual polarization, groups over gaps, multi-component profiles and per-channel noise calibration (un-parked by Jasper "for the χ² stuff").
  • The scenario changed: the array goes on a ship, and the location is the Netherlands. J0437 and Vela never rise there, so the runs use B0329+54 from 52° N, and J0437 from 30° S (Jasper's choice). The default DM became 25, near B0329+54's 26.8, with runs of 0.2 s.
  • A parallel workflow with role agents was tried and stopped within the hour: Jasper prefers to follow the development in one session.
  • Checks of the RFI model: testRFIModel on synthetic data, and testRFIChain on the voltage chain. B0329+54's five components needed finer bins: NBin 2048, and the resolution warning tightened to 1/10 of the template width (Jasper).
  • First scenario runs, with TOA budgets per day (scenario). Jasper's runs of testSimulateFold 1–15 and testRFIModel 1–5 passed.

21 October — profiles, thresholds, validation

  • Overnight runs (Claude's): the scenario's RFI variants (realistic RFI costs about 20–25 % of the TOAs; the calibration brings χ² to 1); J0437's measured profile (about 5× more data per TOA than a single Gaussian); and testRFIChain's narrow-carrier failures diagnosed (the lag cut-off, and detectRFI's false flags).
  • Jasper's decisions: J0437's eight-component profile in runScenario; more lags in rfiProfile and per-channel detectRFI thresholds, both built and tested the same day; the rare low-SNR outliers to be handled in block D's navigation filter; the 100-day runs in the background; the docs keep their DM-5 worked examples.
  • Jasper's MATLAB was down, so Claude ran the validation on a frozen copy of the code.
  • Multi-component pulses in the voltage chain: the generator takes components, and the chain agrees with the fast simulator within about 1 %.
  • Block D started: its design agreed, and D1 (where the receiver is, the delays to the barycentre) and D2 (the predicted phase, the fold that follows it) built and tested (block D). The clock was added to the plan after it had been left out. Two parts of D3 followed: the true arrival of each pulse against the observer's prediction, checked end to end through the fold, and the receiver's clock with white, flicker and random-walk frequency noise. D3 then put the observer's clock estimate into the predictor and the moving receiver's truth into the scenario runs. D4, the navigation fit, was designed around clock synchronisation with extra pulsars; D4a is next.
  • The pipeline overview now shows the fast simulator and the motion prediction around the voltage chain.
  • These docs rewritten for readability (Jasper: the text was "compact and comprehensive, but very hard to comprehend").

22 October — results and validation

  • Overnight runs (Claude's): the validation run on a frozen copy of the code (all eight steps as expected); the first 30-day run with a moving receiver (the 100 km daily sine, 302 TOAs); the 100-day scenario runs (the budgets hold; B0329+54's pulls have an honest core but 1.9 % beyond 3σ, for the navigation fit's outlier model).
  • Jasper's run of all 22 unit tests and of testRFIChain: all pass, with the same numbers. Block D's functions, the per-channel detectRFI thresholds and the extra RFI-profile lags are therefore validated.
  • The receiver chain became concrete: antenna, mixer and a commercial SDR with 18 coherent channels of 400 MHz.

2. Decision log

Every choice that shaped the code, with the reason. "Jasper" marks decisions he made; the others were proposed with the physics and agreed.

30 SepNoise and RFI added at RF after dispersionPhysical order: they enter at the receiver and are never dispersed; dedispersion must treat them as with real data.
30 SepBand-limited dispersion kernel with raised-cosine edgesDelays diverge at f → 0; dispersion is phase-only and commutes with the bandpass, so this is exact in band. A time window would attenuate the band's low end.
30 Sepfref = top of the bandThe top keeps its time, lower frequencies arrive later (as observed); the bulk delay is recorded for later infinite-frequency conversion.
30 SepLinear phase assignment, with weightXNearest-bin assignment had a 0.5 ns bias; linear is unbiased, its neighbour covariance is tracked exactly.
1 OctOff-centre LO (1.3 GHz), 800 MHz (Jasper)A symmetric LO hides a spectral inversion; off-centre it fails visibly.
1 OctOne parameter file; Monte Carlo files in data/mc/Main and experiments cannot drift apart; main never picks up a Monte Carlo realization.
