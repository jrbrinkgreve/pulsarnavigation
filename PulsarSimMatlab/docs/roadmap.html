Design choices: why this order

Excision before the fast simulator (decided 6 Oct)

Alternative: the plan before 6 Oct: the fast simulator first, RFI later.

Jasper's reason: real data contains RFI, excision is mandatory, and simulations without RFI are not realistic. The physics:

  1. At −54 dB one radar pulse ≈ 80,000 pulsar pulses, so excision must blank (weight 0), not subtract.
  2. Excision acts on voltages before dedispersion (a radar pulse is a 2 µs blip there). A fast simulator at profile level can only reproduce its effects, and those must come from a real excision stage.
  3. Blanking needs a data weight per time bin and channel, which changes the fold's data product. Fix the interface before writing a simulator that emulates it.
The fast simulator before motion

Alternative: add motion (D) first, in the voltage simulator.

Why not: at the worst-case design point a J0437 TOA takes many hours, longer than one pass. The catalogue estimate of 6 Oct gave ~20 h. The fast simulator with J0437's measured profile later gave 82–95 h in the worst case, and 16 h at nominal flux (scenario). Hours cannot be simulated at voltage level: the default path writes ~72 GB per second (~61 GB on the full band). The fast simulator draws folded profiles directly, with the exact noise model validated in blocks 1 and A, so detectPulsar and estimateTOA run unchanged on it. Motion (D) is then a hard requirement: folding across passes needs a phase that stays coherent over days.

Block C: fast simulator (20–22 Oct, closed)

Stages. Jasper's decision of 20 Oct: one channel, the full-band equivalent, first; ρ in the simulator's snrDB convention. runScenario converts the catalogue flux to that convention: ρ = S1400/(SEFD·Weq), with Weq the pulse area of the unit-peak profile and SEFD 3.2·10⁵ Jy nominal, 7.4·10⁵ Jy worst case.

  • C1 simulateFold: the mean profile (the periodic Gaussian, smoothed by the time bin and the linear assignment, exact in Fourier space); asymptotic weights n, 2n/3, n/6, or weights copied from a voltage fold; Gaussian noise with the fold's own model (m²/(Bn·binDt), neighbour correlation 0.25, drawn by FFT). foldToTOA runs on it unchanged; 20 h take 0.05 s.
  • C2: validation against the voltage chain (−5 and −20 dB, H0, the weak-regime threshold).
  • C3: time structure: passes, sub-int lengths, scintillation as a flux factor, adaptive sub-int length.
  • C4: channels. C4a (done 20 Oct) gives per-channel folds: NChan, ChannelGain (the true pulse fraction per channel), and NoiseCoeffs (correlated time bins, with closed-form weights W2 = nΣc(L)K(Lδ), WX(d) = nΣc(L)K(d − Lδ), where K is the overlap of two assignment triangles). With the forward taper it reproduces the chain's channel path, including item 3's χ² excess. C4b covers RFI and excision effects; scintillation across the band belongs here too.

The order was C4a → C4b → C3 (20 Oct). The hook for D is a true phase drifting against the ephemeris.

Design draft (6 Oct)

  • Draws the fold struct directly (same fields as foldProfile), plus a separate truth struct.
  • Units: SEFD; baseline 1, pulse S(φ)/SEFD. Mean of bin j: 1 + (Smean/SEFD)·⟨f(φ − Δφ)⟩, smoothed by the assignment kernel and the drift of Δφ(t) = φtrue − φephem within the sub-int (the hook for D).
  • Noise: σ² = m²/(npol·B·binDt) per time bin, phase-bin covariance from the weights, drawn with the Cholesky factor of the banded circular correlation matrix.
  • Weights: asymptotic (W = n, W2 = 2n/3, WX = n/6) or copied from a voltage fold (validation).
  • RFI and excision effects calibrated from block B: mask statistics per RFI type, weight loss, excess noise, corrupted-sub-int rate.
  • Validation: reproduce the block 1 numbers with weights copied from a voltage fold.

Block D: motion and barycentric phase (21–22 Oct, closed)

A TOA is folded across passes, so the fold must follow the receiver's motion. Block D predicts that motion, and turns what the prediction does not explain into a position. Its design was agreed on 21 Oct:

  • an analytic Earth orbit first, and JPL's DE440 at the end of the block;
  • a phase predictor fed by a trajectory, so that a Kalman-like motion predictor can plug in later (sensor fusion for a ship or a deep-space probe);
  • B0329+54's 2-D equatorial site offset as the first result: a 100 km offset should be found to about ±30 km per component in 30 days;
  • rare TOA outliers handled by redundancy in the fit, not by a higher SNR target;
  • the clock as part of the state, after it had been left out of the plans.

done 19 Oct The processing as one function: processIQ

Why: the same stage calls appeared in main, runMonteCarlo, runSNRSweep and runH0 (and in runRFITest and runLockedRadar).

First idea (16 Oct): one function from the sky to the TOAs. Jasper rejected it: real IQ data enter between the simulation and the processing, and the operational processing will run on an FPGA or GPU.

Design (16–19 Oct; Jasper: the boundary at IQ, both front ends). Three roles:

  • simulate the truth, as a stand-in for antenna and ADC/DDC: generate → disperse → addNoiseAndRFI → applyIQmodulation;
  • process: processIQ(info_IQ, rx, ephem, proc, files) runs the filterbank or the full-band dedispersion, through the fold. Then foldToTOA gives the TOAs, detection and the good-TOA rule. They get only the IQ file (or the fold), the receiver settings, the ephemeris and the processing settings, so the ground-truth rule holds by their signatures;
  • evaluate, truth against result: validateTOA and the check plots.

What it allows: the multi-seed Monte Carlo becomes simulate(seed) → processIQ → evaluate, on the default channel path with excision. (Before, that path was validated in pieces and by single-seed runs, never end to end over seeds.) C is validated against that path. D puts a deliberately imperfect ephemeris in, through the separate ephem input. Real recordings go in unchanged, and the FPGA/GPU port is checked against the outputs.
