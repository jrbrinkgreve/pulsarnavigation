Why this order

Excision before the fast simulator (decided 6 Oct)

Jasper's reason: real data contains RFI, excision is mandatory, and simulations without RFI are not realistic. The physics:

  1. At −54 dB one radar pulse ≈ 80,000 pulsar pulses, so excision must blank (weight 0), not subtract.
  2. Excision acts on voltages before dedispersion (a radar pulse is a 2 µs blip there). A fast simulator at profile level can only reproduce its effects, and those must come from a real excision stage.
  3. Blanking needs a data weight per time bin and channel, which changes the fold's data product. Fix the interface before writing a simulator that emulates it.
The fast simulator before motion

At the worst-case design point a J0437 TOA takes ~20 h, longer than one pass. Hours cannot be simulated at voltage level (~61 GB per second). The fast simulator draws folded profiles directly, with the exact noise model validated in blocks 1 and A, so detectPulsar and estimateTOA run unchanged on it. Motion (D) is then a hard requirement: folding across passes needs a phase that stays coherent over days.

  • Spectral kurtosis, whole-channel flags: only if needed.

Block C: fast simulator (design draft, 6 Oct)

  • Draws the fold struct directly (same fields as foldProfile), plus a separate truth struct.
  • Units: SEFD; baseline 1, pulse S(φ)/SEFD. Mean of bin j: 1 + (Smean/SEFD)·⟨f(φ − Δφ)⟩, smoothed by the assignment kernel and the drift of Δφ(t) = φtrue − φephem within the sub-int (the hook for D).
  • Noise: σ² = m²/(npol·B·binDt) per time bin, phase-bin covariance from the weights, drawn with the Cholesky factor of the banded circular correlation matrix.
  • Weights: asymptotic (W = n, W2 = 2n/3, WX = n/6) or copied from a voltage fold (validation).
  • RFI and excision effects calibrated from block B: mask statistics per RFI type, weight loss, excess noise, corrupted-sub-int rate.
  • Validation: reproduce the block 1 numbers with weights copied from a voltage fold.

Block D: motion and barycentric phase

Doppler in the generator and in the fold phase model (~100 µs/s drift at Earth's orbital speed), Earth rotation, station position as input, clock model. A hard requirement since the reference scenario: a TOA is folded across passes.

Longer-term roadmap

  • Block 2 – robustness: DM check from sub-band TOAs; convention assertions; clock jitter.
  • Block 3 – navigation physics: relative motion and acceleration search; multiple pulsars (≥ 4); barycentric corrections (Roemer, Shapiro, Einstein), clock corrections, infinite-frequency conversion (bulkDelayRef); residuals vs timing model; navigation solution (least squares → Kalman).
  • Block 4 – later: 3×3 array with geometric delays and beamforming at IQ level; scattering, DM variations, polarization; generator at high DM.
