Status and roadmap

Where the project stands, the order of the remaining work, and why it is in that order.

Status as of 14 October 2026 (commit 6ad5b4c). Session-by-session history: logs/.

In short

Done: the validated full-band chain (block 1), the channelized front end (A), RFI excision (B, closed for now) and the review week (12–16 Oct), which explained every stage. Now: the processing as one function, processIQ (IQ in, fold out; then foldToTOA), and with it the multi-seed Monte Carlo of the default channel path (A3b). Next: a fast simulator for hours of data (C), then Earth's motion and the phase prediction (D), then several pulsars and the navigation solution (E). The order follows from the reference scenario: a TOA takes ~20 hours, so it must be simulated fast and folded across passes.

Design choices: why this order

Excision before the fast simulator (decided 6 Oct)

Alternative: the plan before 6 Oct: the fast simulator first, RFI later.

Jasper's reason: real data contains RFI, excision is mandatory, and simulations without RFI are not realistic. The physics:

  1. At −54 dB one radar pulse ≈ 80,000 pulsar pulses, so excision must blank (weight 0), not subtract.
  2. Excision acts on voltages before dedispersion (a radar pulse is a 2 µs blip there). A fast simulator at profile level can only reproduce its effects, and those must come from a real excision stage.
  3. Blanking needs a data weight per time bin and channel, which changes the fold's data product. Fix the interface before writing a simulator that emulates it.
The fast simulator before motion

Alternative: add motion (D) first, in the voltage simulator.

Why not: at the worst-case design point a J0437 TOA takes ~20 h, longer than one pass. Hours cannot be simulated at voltage level (~61 GB per second). The fast simulator draws folded profiles directly, with the exact noise model validated in blocks 1 and A, so detectPulsar and estimateTOA run unchanged on it. Motion (D) is then a hard requirement: folding across passes needs a phase that stays coherent over days.

Block B: what remains

  • Block B closed for now (9 Oct, Jasper): the essentials are done; what remained was second-order, tuned against estimated RFI at a pulsar 80,000× brighter than the target. State, open items and ready designs: 2026-10-09_handover-B-closed.md. The periodic mask is off by default.
  • Parked: fix the periodic mask (found in B5e): periodicRFI accepts false emitters when detections are dense and blanks ~30 % of the data (limits). Fix designed: a grid-aware chance calculation, a minimum fraction of predicted pulses detected, trial periods from strong pulses. Added in the review (15 Oct): P1 more test cases (two radars in one channel group, a staggered PRF, more than five emitters); P2 the chance calculation counts the second pulse of the pair that defines a trial period as a chance hit, although it fits by construction (to be handled with the grid-aware chance). Added 16 Oct: P3 the grouping by strongest channel chains across channels, so dense flags join a wide range into one group (9 Oct: channels 29–94) and an emitter blanks the union of its pulses' channels; fix: cap a group's span, or blank only the inliers' main channel cluster. P4 (text only): "the mask does not depend on the noise" means no threshold truncation (a predicted window does not depend on the noise inside it); the windows still depend on the data through the fitted period and phase.
  • Parked: per-channel noise calibration — measure each channel's noise level on the data instead of assuming a flat spectrum (a change to the validated TOA / detection noise model, design note first). Justified by B5e: TOA error bars 3.4 % too small with narrow Inmarsat-like carriers; it also removes the dense false flags that fed the periodic-mask bug.
  • B5e done 9 Oct, validated 12 Oct (decision "measure first"): the realistic L-band scenario as a second list in pipelineParams (receiver, results). Frequencies verified online on 8 Oct: GPS L2 1227.6, GLONASS G2 ~1246, BeiDou B3 1268.52, Galileo E6 1278.75, ATC / long-range radar 1215–1400 (pulses ~1 µs, PRF ~1 kHz, 5–6 rpm), LTE band 32 1452–1496 (EU), Inmarsat downlink 1525–1559, BeiDou B1 1561.1, GPS L1 1575.42, GLONASS G1 ~1598–1606 MHz; 1400–1427 MHz is protected for radio astronomy.
  • Spectral kurtosis, whole-channel flags: only if needed.

Block C: fast simulator (design draft, 6 Oct)

  • Draws the fold struct directly (same fields as foldProfile), plus a separate truth struct.
  • Units: SEFD; baseline 1, pulse S(φ)/SEFD. Mean of bin j: 1 + (Smean/SEFD)·⟨f(φ − Δφ)⟩, smoothed by the assignment kernel and the drift of Δφ(t) = φtrue − φephem within the sub-int (the hook for D).
  • Noise: σ² = m²/(npol·B·binDt) per time bin, phase-bin covariance from the weights, drawn with the Cholesky factor of the banded circular correlation matrix.
  • Weights: asymptotic (W = n, W2 = 2n/3, WX = n/6) or copied from a voltage fold (validation).
  • RFI and excision effects calibrated from block B: mask statistics per RFI type, weight loss, excess noise, corrupted-sub-int rate.
  • Validation: reproduce the block 1 numbers with weights copied from a voltage fold.

Block D: motion and barycentric phase

Doppler in the generator and in the fold phase model (~100 µs/s drift at Earth's orbital speed), Earth rotation, station position as input, clock model. A hard requirement since the reference scenario: a TOA is folded across passes.

Longer-term roadmap

  • Block 2 – robustness: DM check from sub-band TOAs; convention assertions; clock jitter.
  • Block 3 – navigation physics: relative motion and acceleration search; multiple pulsars (≥ 4); barycentric corrections (Roemer, Shapiro, Einstein), clock corrections, infinite-frequency conversion (bulkDelayRef); residuals vs timing model; navigation solution (least squares → Kalman).
  • Block 4 – later: 3×3 array with geometric delays and beamforming at IQ level; scattering, DM variations, polarization; generator at high DM; real IQ recordings and the FPGA/GPU port of the processing, with processIQ as the reference.
