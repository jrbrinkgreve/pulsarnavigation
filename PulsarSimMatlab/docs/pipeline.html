Pipeline overview

Every stage of main.m, the files it writes, and how the two front ends branch and join. Click a box to open its page.

In short

The pipeline first simulates what an antenna would record (sky, then receiver). It then processes that recording the way it would process real data: undo the dispersion, square to power, fold over turns, fit TOAs and test them. There are two processing paths. The full-band path is the validated reference. The channelized path is the default, because RFI excision needs it. Both end in the same fold and TOA fit, and only the final validation looks at the truth.

The map

Read it from top to bottom:

  1. Sky (stages 1–2): make the pulsar's voltage at RF, then send it through the interstellar plasma (dispersion).
  2. Receiver (3–4): add receiver noise and interference, then convert to complex baseband (IQ) at 800 MHz.
  3. Front end (5–6): undo the dispersion and square the voltages to power. Either for the whole band at once (left), or per channel after a filterbank (middle), with RFI excision in between (right).
  4. Fold (7): average the power over turns at the same rotational phase, one profile per sub-integration.
  5. TOA and detection (8): fit the template to each profile, and test whether the pulse is really there.
  6. Validation (9): compare the good TOAs with the truth. This is the only processing-side step that sees it.

Design choices: why the pipeline has this order

Sky → receiver → processing, in physical order

Alternative: add the noise and RFI before dispersion, or simulate the received signal directly.

Why not: noise and RFI enter at the receiver, so they are never dispersed. Dedispersion then applies the inverse chirp to them, exactly as it does to real data: a broadband impulse gets smeared into an inverse sweep, and a carrier is only shifted by its frequency's delay (receiver page).

Complex voltages until detection

Alternative: square to power early, which would shrink the data at once.

Why not: coherent dedispersion needs the phase. RFI blanking and (later) beamforming are linear operations that must also come before squaring. After square-law detection only power is left, and the data are much smaller (sizes).

Two front ends

Alternative: keep only one of them.

Why both: the full-band path (one big dedispersion filter over 400 MHz) was built and validated first. It is simple and exact, but its filter grows with DM (about 15 GB at DM 100) and it has nowhere to remove RFI. The channelized path splits the band into 128 channels first. RFI is then a few samples in a few channels, where it can be found and blanked, and each channel needs only a short dedispersion filter. The full-band path stays as the validated reference: every channel-path result is compared against it.

Excision between the channelizer and dedispersion

Alternative: excise after dedispersion, or on the detected power.

Why not: in a channel, before dedispersion, a 2 µs radar pulse occupies about 8 samples in 2 channels. After dedispersion it is spread over thousands of samples and mixed with good data. So excision works on the raw channel voltages. Dedispersion then spreads each blank over its filter length, so every detected bin is "partly real"; blankingWeights computes exactly how much, and the fold stays unbiased.

Run control in main.m

Each stage group has a switch. true runs it; false reuses the files an earlier run wrote.

SwitchRunsWhen to rerun
runStage.skygenerator + dispersion (the slow part)pulsar, T, L, f_in, DM, band or seed changed
runStage.receivernoise / RFI + IQSNR, RFI, noise seed, fLO or fs changed
runStage.processdedispersion + detection (and channelizer, excision, weights)front end, excision or processing settings changed
runStage.foldfoldingnBin, sub-int length changed
runStage.toaTOAs, detection, validationalways cheap

Skipped stages reload their info from disk. checkConsistency (sky, receiver), checkProcessing and checkFold (13 Oct 2026) warn if those files were made with different parameters or from older inputs. plots.* switch the ground-truth check figures (the IQ, detected and fold checks are for the full-band path only).

Design choice: stages can be skipped, with a warning on mismatch

Alternative: always run everything, or stop with an error when a reused file does not match.

Why not: the sky stage is by far the slowest and does not depend on the noise, so changing the SNR, the RFI or the processing only needs the later stages. A mismatch gives a warning rather than an error, so a deliberate reuse still runs.

Sizes

Disk ≈ L·(3·fin·4 + 2·fs·8 + fout·4) bytes for the full-band files. The channel path adds the channel IQ and dedispersed files (4/3 oversampled), the power file and, with excision, the blanked copies and the weight file (9 float32 per bin and channel). main.m prints its estimate first. At L = 1 s and 800 MHz the full-band files alone are about 61 GB; the default L = 0.1 s needs a tenth of that.
