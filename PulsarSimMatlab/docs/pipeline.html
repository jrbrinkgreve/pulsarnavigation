Why the pipeline has this order

Sky → receiver → processing

The simulator follows the physical order. Noise and RFI are added after dispersion because they enter at the receiver: they are never dispersed. Dedispersion then applies the inverse chirp to them, exactly as with real data: a broadband impulse gets smeared into an inverse sweep; a carrier is only shifted by its frequency's delay.

Complex voltages until detection

Coherent dedispersion needs the phase. RFI blanking and (later) beamforming are linear operations that must also come before squaring. After square-law detection the data rate drops by ~800× and only power is left.

Why two front ends

The full-band path (one big dedispersion filter over 400 MHz) was built and validated first. It is simple and exact, but its filter grows with DM (about 15 GB at DM 100) and it has nowhere to remove RFI. The channelized path splits the band into 128 channels first. RFI is then a few samples in a few channels, where it can be found and blanked, and each channel needs only a short dedispersion filter. The full-band path stays as the validated reference: every channel-path result is compared against it.

Why excision sits between channelizer and dedispersion

In a channel, before dedispersion, a 2 µs radar pulse occupies about 8 samples in 2 channels. After dedispersion it is spread over thousands of samples and mixed with good data. Excision therefore works on the raw channel voltages. Because dedispersion then spreads each blank over its filter length, every detected bin is "partly real"; blankingWeights computes exactly how much, so the fold stays unbiased.

Sizes

Disk ≈ L·(3·fin·4 + 2·fs·8 + fout·4) bytes for the full-band files, plus for the channel path the channel IQ and dedispersed files (4/3 oversampled), the power file and, with excision, the blanked copies and the weight file (9 float32 per bin and channel). main.m prints its estimate first. At L = 1 s and 800 MHz the full-band files alone are about 61 GB; the default L = 0.1 s needs a tenth of that.
