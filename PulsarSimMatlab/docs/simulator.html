Block C · Fast simulator: simulateFold C1–C4b, C3a–b validated, 20 Oct C3c written

Draw the folded profiles directly from their known statistics instead of simulating every voltage sample. Hours of observation then take seconds, and everything after the fold (foldToTOA: TOAs, detection, good TOAs) runs unchanged on the result.

[info, fold, truth] = simulateFold(f0, rhoDB, nTurns, 'SubintPeriods', n, 'NChan', 128, 'ChannelGain', g, 'RFI', prof, ...)

Full interface and help text of simulateFold → · calibration: rfiProfile, runRFIProfile.m · tests: testSimulateFold, expSimVsChain, expSimRFI

In short

A J0437 TOA needs ~20 hours of data; the voltage chain makes ~61 GB per second. But after the fold, everything is described by a few validated numbers per phase bin: the mean (baseline plus the smoothed pulse), the variance (the radiometer equation) and the correlation with the neighbouring bins. simulateFold draws exactly that. It reproduces the voltage chain within its statistics: the full band from the threshold to +20 dB (C2), the channel path including the edge-channel χ² excess of item 3 (C4a), and realistic RFI after excision, from profiles measured on the voltage chain (C4b). 20 hours in 60 s sub-ints take 0.05 s.

The idea

The fold of one sub-int sums many detected time bins per phase bin (about 5 per turn at 100 Hz, 2048 bins and 1 µs, times the turns per sub-int). By then each phase bin is Gaussian, and its mean, variance and covariance with the neighbouring bins follow from the noise model that estimateTOA and detectPulsar already use – the model the voltage chain validated end to end (A3b, runH0). So the simulator draws the fold struct straight from that model, with the same fields and conventions as foldProfile. The true values (the true pulse phase of every sub-int, ρ, the gains, the RFI) go into a separate truth struct; the observer gets only fold and info, which hold nothing foldProfile's would not – the ground-truth rule.

How a fold is drawn

  1. Mean of each phase bin. Baseline 1 (the receiver-noise power) plus the pulse: the generator's unit-peak periodic Gaussian (FWHM 5 % by default), smoothed the way the fold smooths it – averaged over one time bin and spread over ±1 phase bin by the linear assignment – and shifted to the true pulse phase. Computed exactly in Fourier space.
  2. Weights. If the time bins land evenly in phase, a phase bin collects n time bins per sub-int with weight = n, weight2 = 2n/3, weightX = n/6 – the exact averages of the linear split, so neighbouring bins share noise with correlation ¼ (the value runH0 measures). Optionally the weights of a real fold are copied (used for validation).
  3. Noise. Gaussian, with the variance of the radiometer equation, m²/(Bn·binDt)·weight2/weight² (m² rather than 1²: the pulsar's own noise is included), and the neighbour correlation. The correlation matrix of a sub-int is circulant, so the noise is drawn exactly with two FFTs.
  4. Bookkeeping. Sub-int times (tRef, fRef, turnRef) as foldProfile makes them; the truth gets each sub-int's true arrival time (pulseCenters), so validateTOA reads it directly.
The formulas
\[ m_j = 1 + g\,\rho\,\tilde p(\phi_j - \Delta\phi), \qquad \tilde p_k = p_k\,\mathrm{sinc}(k\,f_0\,\Delta t)\,\mathrm{sinc}^2(k/N) \]
\(m_j\)
mean of phase bin \(j\) at phase \(\phi_j = (j-1)/N\), in units of the receiver-noise power
\(\rho\)
pulse-peak signal PSD over receiver-noise PSD (rhoDB, the simulator's snrDB)
\(g\)
signal gain ('SignalGain', default 1; the voltage chain's band taper gives 0.9958)
\(\tilde p_k\)
Fourier coefficients of the smoothed profile; \(p_k\) those of the unit-peak periodic Gaussian
\(\Delta\phi\)
true pulse phase against the ephemeris ('PhaseOffset') [turns]
\(f_0, \Delta t, N\)
spin frequency, time-bin length, phase bins
\[ \operatorname{var}(\mathrm{prof}_j) = \frac{m_j^2}{B_n\,\Delta t}\,\frac{W_2}{W^2}, \qquad \operatorname{corr}(\mathrm{prof}_j, \mathrm{prof}_{j+d}) = \frac{W_X(d)}{W_2}, \qquad \lambda_k = 1 + 2\sum_d r_d \cos\frac{2\pi k d}{N} \]

The noise is z = IFFT(√λ·FFT(e)) for white e, scaled by the standard deviation of each bin.

Channels (C4a)

The default path folds 128 channels separately. Three things differ from the full band:

  1. Correlated time bins. In a 3.125 MHz channel, consecutive 0.96 µs detected bins are correlated (V 0.888, X(1) 0.046, … up to 7 lags; 'NoiseCoeffs', from detectChannels). For time bins spread evenly in phase the weights have a closed form: \( W_2 = n\sum_L c(L)\,K(L\delta) \), \( W_X(d) = n\sum_L c(L)\,K(d - L\delta) \), with \(c(0) = V\), \(c(\pm L) = X(L)\), δ = f0·binDt·N the phase-bin step between time bins and K(s) the overlap of two assignment triangles s bins apart (2/3 − s² + |s|³/2 below 1, (2 − |s|)³/6 up to 2). It matches foldProfile to 2·10⁻⁵.
  2. The true pulse fraction per channel ('ChannelGain', truth): the simulated pulsar passed an 8 MHz band-edge taper that the noise did not, so channels 1/128, 2/127 and 3/126 hold 0.023, 0.417 and 0.960 of a normal channel's pulse (item 3). The observer still fits with gain 1, as main does.
  3. Per-channel noise, independent between channels, each drawn by FFT.

RFI after excision (C4b)

Excision acts on voltages, which the simulator never makes. What it leaves in each channel is measured instead, once, on a short voltage-chain run without pulsar (zero sky + receiver noise + RFI, processIQ with excision; runRFIProfile.m), by rfiProfile, from the detected power of every channel:

  • level Lc: the mean power over the receiver-noise level (constant RFI that excision leaves in raises it; LTE reaches 31× in its channels);
  • the true noise coefficients Vc, Xc(L): a channel partly covered by noise-like RFI has a narrower spectrum and is noisier than the observer's flat model; a channel with a strong carrier is quieter relative to its mean, (1 + 2I)/(1 + I)² (0.36 for the local carrier at I ≈ 4);
  • valid fraction: 1 − the blanked fraction (0.29 % blanked in the realistic scenario).

With 'RFI', channel c's mean is Lc + the pulse, its weights are scaled by the valid fraction, and its noise is drawn with the measured coefficients – while the fold carries the observer's clean model. So the observer's fit makes exactly the mistakes it makes on real data: optimal weighting down-weights the loud channels (the SNR loss), and its error bars are too small in the partly covered channels. These are time-domain numbers, so a profile transfers to any pulsar, phase-bin count and sub-int length. Profiles: data/rfi/profile/rfiProfile_<case>_<L>s.mat for the realistic scenario, the narrow-Inmarsat worst case and noise only, at 0.1 s (runRFITest's exact settings) and 1 s (the steady part, with the rotating radar's beam passage outside the run).

What the model assumes, and how it is checked. The profile is a statistical model, not a recording: the simulator draws fresh noise with the measured statistics in every sub-int, so it assumes (a) the RFI is stationary (its 1 s average holds every hour, every day), (b) the time bins are correlated only over the measured lags (Lmax), so their second moments fix the folded noise, (c) folded profiles are Gaussian, and (d) blanking is not locked to the pulse phase. Three checks:

  1. testRFIModel (synthetic, closed-form truth): rfiProfile recovers level, noise coefficients and valid fraction of a carrier, noise-like narrowband RFI and blanked impulses; folds of that data by foldProfile and simulateFold with the measured profile agree in level, weight, noise relative to the observer's model, neighbour correlation and TOA statistics; and RFI correlated beyond the measured lags is detected (the real fold 3.3× noisier than the model).
  2. expSimRFI part 1: against runRFITest on the voltage chain, within ~1 %.
  3. testRFIChain (planned): the same fold comparison on a voltage-chain run — profile from the first half, fold of the second half — for (b)–(d) with the chain's real RFI and excision.

What the RFI costs (expSimRFI part 2, 1 s profiles, −5 dB, 400 TOAs per case): the realistic scenario loses 10.5 % SNR after excision (σ ×1.12, 25 % more observing time for the same σ), the narrow-Inmarsat worst case 12.2 % (σ ×1.14, 30 %). Error bars stay honest within ~7 %, but the reduced χ² rises to 1.07 / 1.25 — noise the observer's flat channel model misses, which does not shrink at low SNR, so pChi2 flags every TOA with RFI.

Observing schedule (C3a)

Long observations come in passes: the pulsar is above the horizon for some hours a day. With 'Passes' (start and end of each pass, in seconds) and 'SubintTime' (seconds, rounded to whole turns, as foldProfile), the simulator makes complete sub-ints only inside passes, from the complete turns that fit; the gaps hold no data. Times are absolute (tRef = TRef + turnRef/f0), so TOAs from different passes line up on one time axis, and truth.pass says which pass each sub-int belongs to. One pass covering turns 0 … n−1 gives exactly the fold of the plain nTurns interface.

J0437−4715 settings

f0 = 173.6879 Hz (P 5.757452 ms), power-profile FWHM 0.141 ms = 0.0245 turns, NBin 512 (11.2 µs bins, FWHM/12.5: enough by estimateTOA's resolution rule; 20 h × 128 channels in 60 s sub-ints ≈ 1.3 GB of folds), ρ −51.0 dB (worst case) or −47.4 dB (nominal) in the simulator's convention. The scenario assumes two polarizations; with one simulated stream the same information needs Bnoise × 2, for the simulator and the observer alike.

Scintillation (C3b)

The pulsar's flux fades and brightens through interstellar scintillation (physics). Each sub-int and channel gets a true flux factor F that multiplies its pulse: either given ('FluxScale', [nSub × 1] or [nSub × NChan]) or drawn ('Scintillation', with the scintle bandwidth and time):

  1. White complex noise on a time × frequency grid (steps of a tenth of the scintle time; 64 or more points across the band, padded).
  2. Smoothed with Gaussian kernels along both axes, so the field g has the right correlation lengths and unit mean power.
  3. F = |g|²: exponential statistics, intensity correlation 1/e at the scintle time and ½ at the scintle bandwidth.
  4. Taken at each sub-int's mid time and averaged over each channel's frequencies (one channel = the band average).

Switch: off by default – without the option (or with []) the output is bit for bit what it was before C3b, so every validation stays as it was. pipelineParams has scintOn (false), the J0437 values in scint, and scintSim to pass on ([] while off); the voltage chain ignores them.

It has its own random stream, so the noise draws are the same with and without it. Sub-ints should be short against the scintle time (a warning above a fifth of it); long integrations are made by merging short sub-ints (C3c). J0437 at 1.4 GHz: struct('bandwidth', 740e6, 'timescale', 2290). The observer does not know F: its fit has one amplitude per sub-int (which absorbs the fade) and equal channel gains (so frequency structure costs it efficiency).

Adaptive sub-int length (C3c, observer side)

With scintillation the SNR per sub-int changes by large factors from hour to hour, so no fixed sub-int length fits: too long when the pulsar is bright, too short when it is faint. Two observer-side functions, usable on real folds as well as simulated ones:

  1. mergeSubints(fold, info_fold, group) merges runs of consecutive sub-ints exactly: their sums and weights add (only the noise covariance across an internal boundary is missing, as in foldProfile itself; exact for independent time bins).
  2. adaptiveSubints(fold, info_fold, template, Bnoise) walks through short sub-ints and adds them one at a time until the merged profile's matched-filter statistic T0 at the predicted phase reaches the target (default 7, the design rule), then starts a new group. It never merges across a gap between passes; what is left at the end of a pass is a last group below the target, for the good-TOA rule to judge.
Does stopping at SNR 7 bias the TOAs?

A group closes when its noise happens to push T0 up, so the closed groups' SNR is biased up a little. The arrival time is not: with a symmetric template the phase estimate is nearly independent of the amplitude. Checked: at −25 dB (SNR ~1.2 per turn) 109 groups of median 36 turns give TOA pulls with mean +0.15 ± 0.10 and std 0.975; with 2 s scintles 147 groups of median 7 turns, mean −0.08, std 1.04, and the bright stretches close sooner than the faint ones (testSimulateFold 12).

Which ρ

The simulator's ρ, not the scenario page's

rhoDB is the simulator's snrDB: the peak of the Gaussian pulse over the receiver noise, so a simulated fold at −20 dB compares one to one with a voltage-chain run at −20 dB. The reference scenario defines ρ with a pulse width Weq = 2·W50 as a margin, which makes its ρ 2.7 dB lower for the same pulsar (J0437 worst case: −53.7 dB there, −51.0 dB here). A conversion from catalogue flux and SEFD comes later, with the convention stated explicitly.

Design choices

Draw the fold, not the voltages

Alternative: a faster voltage simulator. Why not: even a perfect one makes ~61 GB per second; hours are out of reach. Everything after the fold depends on the voltages only through the fold's statistics, which are validated end to end – so drawing the fold loses nothing the observer could see, and keeps foldToTOA identical for simulated and real folds.

One channel first, channels second (Jasper, 20 Oct)

In the weak regime the channel path's optimally weighted fit is the same estimator as the full band's, about 2 % less efficient (item 3). A single channel kept C1 simple and exactly checkable; the channels (C4a) came once that was validated.

RFI from a measured profile, not from formulas (Jasper, 20 Oct: option a)

Alternative: compute each channel's level and excess noise from the source list (INR, bandwidth, type). Why not: that needs a formula per RFI type (noise, carrier, BPSK, pulsed with excision), each an approximation to validate anyway. A short voltage run measures exactly what the real excision leaves. A 1 s run makes the per-channel noise ratios ~3× more precise than 0.1 s (±0.7 % instead of ±2 %).

The fold carries the observer's model; the noise follows the truth

With RFI, the stored weight2/weightX are what the observer would compute (the clean coefficients), while the drawn noise uses the measured ones. Otherwise the simulation would hide exactly the error-bar mistakes it should show.

Not (yet) in the simulator

  • Time structure still to come: the time variation of rotating radars (C3d), refractive (slow) scintillation.
  • Motion (block D): the true phase drifting against the ephemeris within a sub-int (the hook is 'PhaseOffset' per sub-int).
  • Excision of bright pulses (the chain clips pulse peaks above ~−5 dB) and RFI locked to the pulsar's period.
  • Memory: 128 channels × 2048 bins × 1200 sub-ints is 2.5 GB per array; for hours, channels with identical statistics will be merged into effective channels.
  • Sub-ints are independent (as foldProfile, which does not store the covariance across sub-int boundaries).
