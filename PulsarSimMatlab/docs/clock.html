Stage 4a · The receiver's clock: makeClock validated

Every time stamp comes from the receiver's oscillator, so the oscillator's error goes straight into every TOA. A commercial SDR's oscillator is specified to ±2 ppm. Left uncorrected, that is up to about 170 ms a day.

clk = makeClock([t1 t2], ...)

Full interface and help text of makeClock → · the clock stamps the IQ samples of stage 4 · the observer estimates it in the predictor (stage 7a) and in navigation · block D, since 21 Oct 2026

In short

makeClock draws one realization of the clock's error over time, for the simulator's truth. The observer never sees it. Its navigation filter estimates the clock's offset and rate instead (stage 10).

In the voltage chain every time stamp is exact, and a run lasts seconds. The clock matters over hours and days, so the fast simulator applies it, through trueResiduals.

How it works

  1. The fixed part: an offset at the start, a rate and an ageing drift. A commercial SDR's reference oscillator is specified to ±2 ppm. Its rate can therefore be up to 2·10⁻⁶, which adds 7.2 ms per hour.
  2. The noise: the oscillator's frequency also wanders at random. Its spectrum has three parts, each set by one coefficient: white frequency noise (h₀), flicker (h₋₁) and a random walk (h₋₂).
  3. The grid: the noise is drawn on a 60 s grid over the whole span and is linear in between.

An oscillator's stability is usually given as its Allan deviation σy(τ). It is the typical change of the average frequency from one interval of length τ to the next. White noise makes it fall with τ, flicker keeps it flat, and a random walk makes it grow.

The noise of a commercial SDR's oscillator is not published. It is best measured on the device itself, against a better reference.

The formulas, and how each part is drawn

σy²(τ) = h₀/(2τ) + 2 ln2·h₋₁ + (2π)²/6·h₋₂·τ (the paper's eqs. 6–8).

  • White: the time error takes independent steps of variance h₀Δ/2 per grid step Δ. This is exact.
  • Random walk: the frequency and the time error form a two-state process with covariance q₂·[Δ³/3, Δ²/2; Δ²/2, Δ] per step, q₂ = 2π²h₋₂. This is exact, and it is the model the Kalman filter uses.
  • Flicker: no finite-state model is exact. The step-averaged frequency is synthesized by FFT with the spectrum h₋₁/f, over twice the span, so that the FFT never joins the two ends. It is then integrated.

Between grid nodes the left-out noise is a white-noise bridge of √(h₀Δ/8): 0.12 ns for the crystal at 60 s.

Design choices

Flicker included, by FFT

Alternative: only white and random-walk noise, the two that a two-state Kalman model describes exactly.

Why not: for the paper's crystal, flicker is the largest of the three noises from 1 s to 100 s. After 200 s it is 42 % of the time error's variance. Leaving it out would make the clock look better than it is.
