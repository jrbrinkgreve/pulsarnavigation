Stage 9 · Validation and check functions

The functions that compare the pipeline with the simulation's ground truth. They are the only processing-side code allowed to read info_gen, info_disp and info_rx, and they are never used to make a result, only to check one.

How a result counts as validated

Three kinds of check run throughout the project, and most tests use all three:

  • Regression: a changed function reproduces old outputs bit for bit where nothing should change.
  • Algebra: the code equals the formula computed independently (often a slow explicit matrix), to machine precision (~10⁻¹⁵).
  • Physics / statistics: measured noisy quantities match predictions within n·σ, with σ predicted, not guessed.

A result is marked validated only after Jasper's own MATLAB run.

Why predicted σ, not guessed tolerances

A tolerance like "within 1 %" proves nothing unless you know how large the scatter should be. Every statistical test therefore predicts its own σ from the physics (the number of samples, the noise model) and passes at a few σ. A test that passes at 4σ of a predicted scatter says the code and the theory agree; a test that passes a guessed tolerance only says the number was not far off.

Why the truth is kept out of the processing

The check functions are the only processing-side code that may read the simulation's truth, and they never feed a result. Every stage that produces a TOA must work unchanged on real data, where there is no truth to read (the ground-truth rule). Keeping the two apart is what makes a validated result mean something for real data.

validateTOA validated

val = validateTOA(toa, info_gen, 'Plot', true, 'Label', '')

Full interface and help text of validateTOA →

Matches each valid TOA to the nearest true pulse centre (info_gen.pulseCenters) and reports:

  • errors and normalized errors z = error / toaErr;
  • mean ± its expected scatter; rms vs the rms of the predicted errors (the ratio, should be 1);
  • χ² of z with both tail probabilities (gammainc);
  • fractions |z| < 1 and < 2 (expected 68.3 % and 95.4 %);
  • the total-fold offset in σ.

Figure: errors with ±1σ bars, histogram vs N(0,1), Q-Q plot, amplitude and SNR per sub-int. For Monte Carlo, pass struct arrays [toa1 toa2 …] and [info_gen1 …]: all TOAs are pooled.

Reading the numbers
  • Ratio = rms of actual errors / rms of stated errors. 1 = honest error bars; < 1 pessimistic; > 1 optimistic. With n TOAs its own uncertainty is about 1/√(2n): ±0.075 for 90 TOAs, ±25 % for 8.
  • Q-Q plot: sorted z against the N(0,1) quantiles Φ⁻¹((k−½)/n). On the line y = x: correct. Steeper: error bars too small. Shifted: bias. S-shaped tails: outliers. It uses no binning and shows the tails, where outliers live.

In main.m it runs on all valid TOAs and again on the good TOAs only, if some were rejected.

expectedPowerModel written

M = expectedPowerModel(info_gen, info_disp, info_IQ, info_dedisp, info_rx)

Full interface and help text of expectedPowerModel →

The ground-truth mean and variance of the detected power, shared by the plot checks, and the best achievable precision.

How it works

  1. A frequency grid across the band (200,001 points) with the simulator's dispersion taper Wf and the observer's dedispersion taper Wi.
  2. The baseband power spectral density is S = ss·p(t)·Wf²Wi² + sn·Wi²: the pulsar passed both tapers, the receiver noise only Wi (it was added after dispersion).
  3. Five integrals: Js = ∫(WfWi)², Jn = ∫Wi² for the mean powers, and ∫(WfWi)⁴, ∫Wf²Wi⁴, ∫Wi⁴ for the variance.
  4. Mean powers Ps(t) = sigScale·p(t), Pn; variance of a bin of length dt:
\[ \operatorname{var} = \frac{c_{ss}\,P_s^2 + 2\,c_{sn}\,P_s P_n + c_{nn}\,P_n^2}{dt}, \qquad c_{ss} = \frac{\int (W_f W_i)^4}{J_s^2}, \quad c_{sn} = \frac{\int W_f^2 W_i^4}{J_s J_n}, \quad c_{nn} = \frac{\int W_i^4}{J_n^2} \]
\(\operatorname{var}\)
variance of the detected power of one time bin [V⁴]
\(P_s\), \(P_n\)
mean pulsar power at that time, \(\texttt{sigScale}\cdot p(t)\), and mean receiver-noise power [V²]
\(dt\)
length of the time bin [s]
\(c_{ss}, c_{sn}, c_{nn}\)
self-noise, cross and noise coefficients [1/Hz]; their inverses are noise bandwidths (M.css …)
\(W_f\), \(W_i\)
the simulator's dispersion taper and the observer's dedispersion taper, as functions of frequency
\(J_s\), \(J_n\)
\(\int (W_f W_i)^2\) and \(\int W_i^2\): the bandwidths that set the mean powers [Hz]

This is Isserlis' theorem with S = pulsar + noise: squaring the sum gives a self-noise term, a cross term and a noise term. For a flat band all c = 1/B, and var = (Ps + Pn)²/(B·dt). The formula was verified numerically, within 1 %, for 0 ≤ ρ ≤ 100.

Best achievable

The best achievable precision follows from the Fisher information. With \(v = c_{ss}P_s^2 + 2c_{sn}P_sP_n + c_{nn}P_n^2\), the variance above times \(dt\):

\[ \mathrm{SNR}^2 = \int \frac{P_s^2}{v}\,dt, \qquad \frac{1}{\sigma_\mathrm{TOA}^2} = \int \frac{P_s'^2}{v}\,dt, \qquad P_s' = \frac{dP_s}{dt} \]

These give M.snrPulse and M.toaErrPulse. Without noise they are NaN, because the integrals diverge in the tails. At −5 dB they are 96.5 and 2.892 µs, also checked against a Python port. RFI is not in the model; it shows up as an excess in the checks. (Added 5 Oct 2026.)

Why compute the exact optimum

Without it you cannot tell whether FFTFIT is 2 % or 0.5 % from optimal (it is 0.5 % at −5 dB). The integrals ∫W² for the mean and (∫W²)²/∫W⁴ for the variance are also the prototype of the narrow-channel and blanking noise models. On real data with ripple, slopes and notches the difference can be 10–30 %.

Status written in the notes' file list: it is used and cross-checked, but not formally validated as a stage.

The centroid cross-check, and the +3.7 µs that was not a bias

The plot checks estimate the pulse position independently with a centroid c = Σt·Q/ΣQ over ±4σ (Q = power minus baseline). A noise blip far from the centre moves it more (the "lever arm"), and the window wings hold almost no pulse but full noise, so the centroid is ~2× noisier than FFTFIT, which weights by the slope. With noise seed 43 it came out +3.7 µs; five seeds gave −0.58 ± 0.93 µs: a 1.8σ fluctuation of one seed, closed on 6 Oct.
