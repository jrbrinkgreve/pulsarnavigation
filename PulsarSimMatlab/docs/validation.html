Stage 9 · Validation and check functions

The functions that compare the pipeline with the simulation's ground truth. They are the only processing-side code allowed to read info_gen, info_disp and info_rx, and they are never used to make a result, only to check one.

Stages 1–8 produce good TOAs. This stage asks whether they are right: the scatter of (TOA − truth) must match the stated error bars (validateTOA), the error bars must be close to the best possible (expectedPowerModel), and each stage must show its expected signal (the plot checks). Over hours and days, the fast simulator and the navigation fit are judged the same way, against their own truth.

9.1 How a result counts as validated

A result counts as validated when a test predicts its own scatter and the code lands inside it, in Jasper's own run. Three kinds of check run throughout the project, and most tests use all three. The first is regression: a changed function reproduces old outputs bit for bit where nothing should change. The second is algebra: the code equals the formula computed independently (often a slow explicit matrix), to machine precision (~10⁻¹⁵). The third is physics and statistics: measured noisy quantities match predictions within n·σ, with σ predicted, not guessed.

A result is marked validated only after Jasper's own MATLAB run.

The scatter is predicted, not guessed, because a tolerance like "within 1 %" proves nothing unless one knows how large the scatter should be. Every statistical test therefore predicts its own σ from the physics (the number of samples, the noise model) and passes at a few σ. A test that passes at 4σ of a predicted scatter says the code and the theory agree; a test that passes a guessed tolerance only says the number was not far off.

The truth is kept out of the processing. The check functions are the only processing-side code that may read it, and they never feed a result. Every stage that produces a TOA must work unchanged on real data, where there is no truth to read (the ground-truth rule). Keeping the two apart is what makes a validated result mean something for real data.

9.2 Long runs and navigation: judged against their truth

The same separation holds for long runs. The fast simulator keeps its truth in a separate struct, and trueResiduals gives the true pulse phase of a moving receiver (fast simulator). The navigation results are judged against that truth in three ways.

The first is the true residuals themselves. expNavFit fits the noise-free true residuals first, and they give the 100 km offset as 99.994 km. The fit and the truth agree, so any error after that comes from the TOAs. The second is pulls at fixed times, over many realizations. A smoothed track's errors are correlated along the whole track, so the pulls of all TOAs of one run are not independent. testNavFilter and the ship runs therefore take the pulls at four fixed times (in the ship runs days 3, 7, 10 and 14) and pool them over realizations (Figure 9.1). The third is an ideal twin: the same smoother runs on ideal TOAs, the truth averaged over the same sub-ints, plus Gaussian noise of the stated σ. If the real filter's errors match the twin's, the TOA chain adds nothing; if not, the difference is the chain's (navigation).

A single ship run is far noisier than its pulls suggest, because its errors are correlated along the track. The filter is therefore judged over many seeds, never on one run.

9.3 validateTOA validated

val = validateTOA(toa, info_gen, 'Plot', true, 'Label', '')

Full interface and help text of validateTOA →

validateTOA matches each valid TOA to the nearest true pulse centre (info_gen.pulseCenters) and reports the errors and the normalized errors z = error / toaErr; the mean ± its expected scatter; the rms against the rms of the predicted errors (the ratio, which should be 1); χ² of z with both tail probabilities (gammainc); the fractions |z| < 1 and < 2 (expected 68.3 % and 95.4 %); and the total-fold offset in σ.

Its figure shows the errors with ±1σ bars, a histogram against N(0,1), a Q-Q plot, and the amplitude and SNR per sub-int. For a Monte Carlo, pass struct arrays [toa1 toa2 …] and [info_gen1 …]: all TOAs are pooled. In main.m it runs on all valid TOAs and again on the good TOAs only, if some were rejected.

Reading the numbers

The ratio is the rms of the actual errors over the rms of the stated errors: 1 means honest error bars, < 1 pessimistic ones, > 1 optimistic ones. With n TOAs its own uncertainty is about 1/√(2n): ±0.075 for 90 TOAs, ±25 % for 8.

The Q-Q plot shows the sorted z against the N(0,1) quantiles Φ⁻¹((k−½)/n). On the line y = x the errors are correct; a steeper line means error bars too small, a shifted one a bias, and S-shaped tails outliers. It uses no binning and shows the tails, where outliers live.

9.4 expectedPowerModel: the best achievable written

M = expectedPowerModel(info_gen, info_disp, info_IQ, info_dedisp, info_rx)

Full interface and help text of expectedPowerModel →

An honest error bar is not yet a good one. expectedPowerModel gives the ground-truth mean and variance of the detected power, which the plot checks share, and from them the best achievable precision. Without it one cannot tell whether FFTFIT is 2 % or 0.5 % from optimal (it is 0.5 % at −5 dB).

  1. A frequency grid across the band (200,001 points) with the simulator's dispersion taper Wf and the observer's dedispersion taper Wi.
  2. The baseband power spectral density is S = ss·p(t)·Wf²Wi² + sn·Wi²: the pulsar passed both tapers, the receiver noise only Wi (it was added after dispersion).
  3. Five integrals: Js = ∫(WfWi)², Jn = ∫Wi² for the mean powers, and ∫(WfWi)⁴, ∫Wf²Wi⁴, ∫Wi⁴ for the variance.
  4. Mean powers Ps(t) = sigScale·p(t), Pn; and the variance of a bin of length dt:
(9.1)\[ \operatorname{var} = \frac{c_{ss}\,P_s^2 + 2\,c_{sn}\,P_s P_n + c_{nn}\,P_n^2}{dt}, \qquad c_{ss} = \frac{\int (W_f W_i)^4}{J_s^2}, \quad c_{sn} = \frac{\int W_f^2 W_i^4}{J_s J_n}, \quad c_{nn} = \frac{\int W_i^4}{J_n^2} \]
\(\operatorname{var}\)
variance of the detected power of one time bin [V⁴]
\(P_s\), \(P_n\)
mean pulsar power at that time, \(\texttt{sigScale}\cdot p(t)\), and mean receiver-noise power [V²]
\(dt\)
length of the time bin [s]
\(c_{ss}, c_{sn}, c_{nn}\)
self-noise, cross and noise coefficients [1/Hz]; their inverses are noise bandwidths (M.css …)
\(W_f\), \(W_i\)
the simulator's dispersion taper and the observer's dedispersion taper, as functions of frequency
\(J_s\), \(J_n\)
\(\int (W_f W_i)^2\) and \(\int W_i^2\): the bandwidths that set the mean powers [Hz]

This is Isserlis' theorem with S = pulsar + noise: squaring the sum gives a self-noise term, a cross term and a noise term. For a flat band all c = 1/B, and var = (Ps + Pn)²/(B·dt). The formula was verified numerically, within 1 %, for 0 ≤ ρ ≤ 100. The best achievable precision then follows from the Fisher information. With \(v = c_{ss}P_s^2 + 2c_{sn}P_sP_n + c_{nn}P_n^2\), the variance above times \(dt\):

(9.2)\[ \mathrm{SNR}^2 = \int \frac{P_s^2}{v}\,dt, \qquad \frac{1}{\sigma_\mathrm{TOA}^2} = \int \frac{P_s'^2}{v}\,dt, \qquad P_s' = \frac{dP_s}{dt} \]

These give M.snrPulse and M.toaErrPulse. Without noise they are NaN, because the integrals diverge in the tails. At −5 dB they are 96.5 and 2.892 µs, also checked against a Python port. RFI is not in the model; it shows up as an excess in the checks. The integrals ∫W² for the mean and (∫W²)²/∫W⁴ for the variance are also the prototype of the narrow-channel and blanking noise models. On real data with ripple, slopes and notches the difference from a flat band can be 10–30 %.

The centroid cross-check, and the +3.7 µs that was not a bias

The plot checks estimate the pulse position independently with a centroid c = Σt·Q/ΣQ over ±4σ (Q = power minus baseline). A noise blip far from the centre moves it more (the "lever arm"), and the window wings hold almost no pulse but full noise, so the centroid is ~2× noisier than FFTFIT, which weights by the slope. With noise seed 43 it came out +3.7 µs; five seeds gave −0.58 ± 0.93 µs: a 1.8σ fluctuation of one seed.

Summary
  • A result is validated when a test predicts its own scatter and the code lands inside it, in Jasper's own run.
  • Only the check functions read the truth, and they never feed a result.
  • validateTOA judges TOAs by the ratio of actual to stated scatter and by Q-Q plots; long runs are judged at fixed times over many realizations, against an ideal twin.
  • expectedPowerModel gives the best achievable SNR and TOA error (Eq. 9.2), against which FFTFIT is 0.5 % from optimal.

The validated TOAs now go to the last stage, navigation, which turns them into a position.
