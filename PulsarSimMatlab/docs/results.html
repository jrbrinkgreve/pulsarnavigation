Validation record

Every major measured result against its prediction, in time order, with what each one established. The complete tables are in logs/current_project_notes.md §7.

Labels used below: block 1 (the full-band chain) was done in phases; phase D was the SNR sweep, and phase E the detector and the good-TOA rule. Blocks A (channelized front end), B (RFI excision), C (the fast simulator) and D (motion, the clock and navigation) were done in numbered steps (A1–A4, B1–B6, C1–C4, D1–D5); the roadmap lists them.

In short

Every check so far tells the same story. When a sub-integration has an SNR of about 6 or more, the stated TOA error bars are honest to within a few percent, from weak signals to strong ones. The noise model is complete, and the detector's false-alarm rate is as designed. Below that SNR some TOAs lock onto noise peaks, and the good-TOA rule removes them. Interference is the exception: a radar destroys TOAs unless it is excised.

The fast simulator reproduces the voltage chain, from the detection threshold to high SNR, with and without RFI. Over 100 simulated days the core of the error bars stays honest, but 1.9 % of B0329+54's TOAs lie beyond 3σ, so the navigation fit expects outliers. With motion and a drifting clock, the navigation fit and filter find the position with honest error bars. B0329+54 alone gives ±30 km per 30 days for a fixed site. A ship with a calibrated start stays within 73 km rms of its track at nominal flux. At worst-case flux it is at 298 km rms, against 372 km for dead reckoning (navigation; Claude's run).

  • Lopsided channels are understood. A flat-channel formula predicted exactly which 8 channels stand out (LTE edges, Inmarsat block edges, GNSS L1) and the 0.39 %. χ² overstates the problem: every channel counts equally in χ², but the TOA fit weights a channel by 1/level², and the lopsided channels contain the RFI, so their weight is small.
  • Worst case: narrow noise-like carriers about as strong as their channel's noise. Such a channel keeps about ¼ of its weight, with about 3.5× the model's noise. Result: χ² 1.22, and error bars 3.4 % too small. That is enough to matter for navigation, so the per-channel noise calibration is worth doing (limits). It was done on 20 Oct (noiseCal). On the chain (DM 25, 0.2 s) χ² goes from 1.24 to 0.99, and σ from 3.379 to 3.419 µs.
  • Cost of the environment: −11 % SNR (~25 % longer observing), mostly LTE band 32 (~14 channels effectively lost). The +40 dB radar: all its pulses blanked, none of its energy missed.
  • Bug found in the periodic mask. With periodicMask on (main's default until 9 Oct), the same cases blank 29–32 % of the data, and the SNR drops to 69 and 66. periodicRFI accepts false emitters at ~14.1 kHz, from the dense false flags of the lopsided channels (limits). The mask is off by default since 9 Oct. A fix is designed, not implemented.

15. Block D: from TOAs to a position (22 Oct)

These runs give the fast simulator the truth of a receiver that is not where the observer thinks, and a clock that drifts (the simulator's truth). The observer then fits its position. All are Claude's runs; the functions they use are validated (section 14).

16. Algorithm ports and run times

NumPy ports, made before the MATLAB runs, checked the algorithms:

  • dispersion kernel: group delays exact to 10⁻⁴ µs, leakage ~10⁻¹², overlap-add equal to direct convolution;
  • forward → IQ → inverse round trip: ~2·10⁻⁶; IQ tones: ~1.5·10⁻⁶;
  • detection: channel mapping and Parseval exact;
  • fold: the indexing, and the bias of nearest-bin against linear assignment;
  • FFTFIT Monte Carlo: bias < 0.1 ps noise-free; σ predicted 0.469 µs against 0.46–0.47 µs measured (0.383 µs without the covariance);
  • the variance formula for mixed signal and noise: within 1 %.

Run times (L = 1 s, M-series Mac, 24 GB): dispersion 81.5 s, IQ 46 s, dedispersion 7.4 s, detection 1.2 s, fold 0.05 s; the generator is dominated by random-number generation.
