info = applyInverseDispersion(inFile, outFile, fs, fLO, DM, fLow, fHigh, 'RefFreq', refFreq)

Full interface and help text of applyInverseDispersion →

In short

It undoes interstellar dispersion, exactly. It filters the complex voltage with the inverse of the chirp that stage 2 applied, so every frequency comes out at the time it would have at 1.6 GHz. It uses only the catalogue DM and the receiver settings.

Worked numbers at DM 5 (kept for visual clarity)

The figures, FFT sizes and run times in this section are worked out at DM 5, where the figures are easier to read. The default DM is 25. The method is the same.

The physics

For every FFT bin, with RF frequency f = fLO + fbb inside the band, the filter is

\[ H_\mathrm{inv}(f) = W(f)\, \exp\!\left(-i 2\pi K\,\mathrm{DM}\, \frac{(f - f_\mathrm{ref})^2}{f\, f_\mathrm{ref}^2}\right), \qquad H_\mathrm{inv}(f) = 0 \text{ outside the band} \]
\(H_\mathrm{inv}(f)\)
the inverse dispersion filter, one value per FFT bin
\(f\)
RF frequency of the bin: \(f_\mathrm{LO} + f_\mathrm{bb}\) [Hz], with \(f_\mathrm{bb}\) its baseband frequency
\(f_\mathrm{ref}\)
radio reference frequency, whose timing is left unchanged [Hz] (RefFreq, default fHigh = 1.6 GHz)
\(K\)
dispersion constant, 4.148808·10¹⁵ s Hz² pc⁻¹ cm³ (= 4.148808·10³ s MHz² pc⁻¹ cm³)
\(\mathrm{DM}\)
dispersion measure from the catalogue [pc cm⁻³] (ephem.DM = 5)
\(W(f)\)
the observer's band window: 1 inside, raised-cosine (sin²) edges, 0 outside
  • It is the forward chirp with the opposite sign. Its group delay is −(τ(f) − τ(fref)): lower frequencies are moved earlier, by up to 6.3 ms.
  • Moving something earlier needs input from the future, so the filter is non-causal: each output needs up to 6.3 ms of later input.
  • Because it acts on the voltage, it removes dispersion exactly at every frequency, even inside a narrow channel. What it does to noise and RFI is shown on the receiver page: impulses become inverse sweeps.

How it works

  1. Check the inputs: the band must lie inside fLO ± fs/2, and fref inside the band (unless AllowRefOutsideBand).
  2. Work out how far the filter reaches: into the future, the whole sweep plus a guard (nFuture); into the past, only the guard (nPast), because fref is the top of the band.
  3. Write the filter directly on the FFT grid, for the bins inside the band, with the phase in double precision. The data are complex, so there is no symmetry to use.
  4. Check the reach: one inverse FFT of the filter shows how much of its response falls outside [−nFuture, +nPast]. That leakage must be tiny (about 10⁻¹² with the default guard; warning above 10⁻⁶). This proves the overlap is long enough.
  5. Stream with overlap-save: one buffer holds [past | outputs | future]. Each FFT block keeps only the outputs whose whole window was inside the buffer, then the buffer shifts on.
  6. Mark the fully supported outputs: samples nPast + 1 to N − nFuture saw their complete sweep.
Exact formulas and numbers
  • nFuture = max(⌈(τ(fLow) − τ(fref))·fs⌉, 0) + g, nPast = max(⌈(τ(fref) − τ(fHigh))·fs⌉, 0) + g, guard g = ⌈20/edge width·fs⌉ (2.5 µs). At 800 MHz: about 5.04 million and 2000 samples. In the original 500 MHz run: 3,152,472 and 1250.
  • FFT size: powers of two from 2nextpow2(2·overlap) up to the whole file, the one with the lowest cost nblocks·Nfft·log₂Nfft within MaxMemoryGB (4), at 56 bytes per FFT sample. Original 1 s run at 500 MHz: 2²⁶, 95 % useful, 8 blocks, 3.76 GB, 7.4 s. For the 0.1 s file at 800 MHz the rule gives 2²⁵ (85 % useful, 3 blocks, 1.9 GB).
  • Zeros before the start and after the end of the file. fullySupported = [nPast + 1, N − nFuture].

AllowRefOutsideBand

Added for the channelized front end, so that every 3.125 MHz channel can be referenced to the top of the whole band.

  • Default false: fref must lie in [fLow, fHigh]. True: any fref > 0.
  • The filter formula is exact for any fref. Its phase splits into the 1/f dispersion term, a linear term (a pure delay) and a constant. A reference above the band only adds a pure advance, so all the overlap moves to the future side.
  • nFuture and nPast use max(⌈…⌉, 0) + g, which changes nothing inside the band. Regression: the full-band path reproduces test_IQ_dedispersed.dat bit for bit.
History

AllowRefOutsideBand was added on 6 Oct 2026, for the channelized front end.

Design choices

Coherent dedispersion of the voltage, not shifting sub-bands

Alternative: incoherent dedispersion: split the band into channels, detect each, and shift each channel by its delay.

Why not: the delay also changes inside each channel, so a shift leaves a smear. In a 3.125 MHz channel at the bottom of the band the smear is 372 µs at DM 25 (75 µs at DM 5), and 1.5 ms at DM 100. At DM 100 that is more than a millisecond pulsar's whole pulse. Filtering the voltage removes the sweep at every frequency, with no smear at all. This is why the channel path also dedisperses each channel coherently.

The same constant, DM, reference and taper as the forward stage

An exact round trip needs the same constant, DM, reference frequency and sign. A different RefFreq would leave a constant shift τ(fref,fwd) − τ(fref). That is harmless for relative timing, but it changes what a TOA means. The taper W (8 MHz raised-cosine edges) keeps the filter's response short, so the guard and the leakage check work.

The simulation and the observer share one refFreq; that is noted on the roadmap (review item R-D2).

Overlap-save here, overlap-add in the forward stage

Both are exact. Overlap-save suits a filter with a very lopsided, non-causal reach (6.3 ms into the future, a few µs into the past): one buffer holds past, outputs and future, and each block keeps only the outputs that saw a complete window. The forward filter only reaches forward, where overlap-add is the natural fit (sky page).

A leakage check instead of a designed kernel

Stage 2 builds a kernel in time and cuts it to length. Here the filter is applied directly as H on each block's FFT grid, so there is nothing to cut. What can go wrong is the opposite: a response that reaches further than the overlap and wraps around inside the block. The leakage check measures exactly that, on every run.

History

Until 27 Oct 2026 the check summed in single precision. Near 1, single precision resolves only 6·10⁻⁸, so it reported 0 or a multiple of 6·10⁻⁸ instead of about 10⁻¹². testApplyInverseDispersion found it; the sums are now in double, and the output data did not change (bit for bit).

Only fully supported data go on

The last 6.3 ms lack part of their sweep: stage 2 dropped the energy that would arrive after the end of the file, and a real observation stops the same way. Folding those samples would give a profile that is too weak at low frequencies there. So the fully supported range travels with the data, and the fold uses only that (timeline figure above).

6 · detectPower validated

info = detectPower(inFile, outFile, fs, fLO, f_out, 'FullySupported', info_dedisp.fullySupported)

Full interface and help text of detectPower →

In short

It squares the dedispersed voltage, |z|² = I² + Q², and averages it over time bins of 1 µs. Here the data rate collapses, from 800 million complex samples per second to 1 million power values, so everything after this is cheap.

The physics

After dedispersion the pulse is still noise; only its variance carries the signal (see the voltage figure). |z|² estimates the variance. For noise whose variance follows a known profile, a profile-weighted sum of |z|² is the optimal (Neyman–Pearson) test statistic. So the optimal order is: square, then fold, then match the template.

Each 1 µs bin averages about Bn·Δt ≈ 390 independent samples, so its noise is 1/√390 ≈ 5.1 % of its mean (the radiometer equation).

How it works

  1. Bin length: binLen = round(fs/fout) samples, a whole number (800 at 800 MHz).
  2. Square and average: I² + Q² straight from the raw read (no complex arrays, no square root), summed in double, divided by binLen. A trailing partial bin is dropped.
  3. Time stamp: each bin is stamped at the centroid of its samples, tk = binTime0 + (k−1)·binDt.
  4. Support: fullySupportedBins are the bins whose samples are all fully supported.
Exact formulas, and the old channelized mode
  • tk = T0 + ((k−1)·binLen + (binLen−1)/2)/fs; binTime0 = (binLen − 1)/(2fs) = 0.4994 µs at 800 MHz.
  • Channelized mode (NChan > 1 with Band): rectangular, non-overlapping FFT frames whose length divides binLen (≥ 8 FFT bins per channel); |Z|² summed into channels by one matrix (Parseval normalization), so the channels of a full-baseband band sum exactly to the total. Up to ~32 clean channels at 1 µs bins. The 128-channel front end uses channelizeIQ instead.

Design choices

Square first, then fold, then match the template

The pulsar is noise, so its information is in the variance. Squaring turns variance into a mean; folding adds up the turns; the template match weights each phase by how much pulse it holds. For variance-modulated Gaussian noise that order is optimal (Neyman–Pearson). Everything linear (dedispersion, RFI blanking, later beamforming) must happen before the square, because after it the phase is gone.

Average every sample into the bin (integrate and dump)

Alternative: keep one squared sample per bin, as a simple power detector followed by a slow ADC would.

Why not: that throws away most of the data, and its SNR then grows with fout. Averaging all samples reaches the radiometer limit at any fout: the information is set by bandwidth × time, not by the bin rate (tests/expSamplingRate.m shows this). It also matters for the small-antenna hardware: integrate before decimating.

1 µs bins (fout = 1 MHz)

Because every bin averages all its samples, a finer bin does not add sensitivity; fout is a resolution and cost knob. Two limits set it:

  • bins must be much finer than the pulse, below about W/5 = 100 µs, or the error bars become too small (figure);
  • with NBin = 2048 and one turn per sub-int, every phase bin needs data: bins of at most 4.9 µs.

1 µs is far inside both, and still collapses the data rate 800-fold. A trial with 10 µs bins and 2048 phase bins gave no TOAs at all. The fold now warns about that (foldProfile:coverage).

The bin is stamped at its centroid, not its start

Stamping at the start would label every bin half a bin before its content, so every TOA would come out half a bin (0.5 µs) early. The centroid of a symmetric boxcar is the unbiased time. With linear phase assignment in the fold (fold) it carries through to the TOA.
