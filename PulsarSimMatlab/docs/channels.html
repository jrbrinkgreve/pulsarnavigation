Stages 5b–6b · Channelized front end

The default processing path since 8 October 2026 (block A): split the band into 128 channels of 3.125 MHz, dedisperse each channel coherently to a common reference, and detect each channel with an exact model of its noise.

Selected with frontEnd = 'channels'. RFI excision (excision = true, default) sits between channelizeIQ and dedisperseChannels; see RFI excision.

Why a channelized front end

The decision (6 Oct 2026)
  • RFI excision needs it. Real data contains RFI; at −54 dB one radar pulse outweighs ~80,000 pulsar pulses, so it must be blanked on the voltages before dedispersion. In a 3.125 MHz channel a 2 µs radar pulse is ~8 samples in 2 channels, easy to find and cheap to remove. In the full band there is no place to do this.
  • Memory at high DM. One full-band dedispersion filter grows with the sweep: about 15 GB at DM 100. Per channel, the filter only spans that channel's own (much shorter) sweep at a 4.17 MHz rate: ~10⁴ samples instead of ~10⁸.
  • Sub-band TOAs later (a DM check from per-channel arrival times, block 2).
  • The full-band path stays as the validated reference. With all options off, every changed function reproduces its old output bit for bit.

Coherent dedispersion per channel (not incoherent) keeps the advantage of the full-band path: no smearing inside a channel.

5a · channelizeIQ validated

info_chan = channelizeIQ(inFile, outBase, fs, fLO, fLow, fHigh, 'ChanWidth', 3.125e6, 'T0', info_IQ.t0)

Full interface and help text of channelizeIQ →

Purpose

Split the IQ stream into channels that tile [fLow, fHigh] exactly, each a complex voltage stream at a reduced rate.

What a channel is

Channel j is exactly what a separate receiver would give: mix down by its centre fc,j = fLow + (j − ½)·ChanWidth (LO phase 0 at input sample 0), low-pass with zero phase, keep every D-th sample:

yj(m) = Σn h(mD − n)·x(n)·e−i2π(fc,j − fLO)n/fs
  • Sample m is at t0 + mD/fs (group delay removed, no TOA offset), and RF = fc,j + fbb (no inversion): each channel file is an ordinary IQ file with fLO = fc,j and rate fs/D. That is why the validated applyInverseDispersion can be reused on it unchanged.
  • Unit DC gain: tone amplitudes and passband power density are preserved, and the channels' useful bands add up to the IQ band power.

Oversampling 4/3

At fs = 800 MHz: K = fs/ChanWidth = 256 channel slots, decimation D = K·3/4 = 192, channel rate 4.17 MHz. The prototype filter is flat to ±1.5625 MHz (± half a channel) and reaches its stopband at fs/D − ChanWidth/2 = 2.604 MHz.

Why oversample instead of a critically sampled filterbank

With D = K (critical sampling) the transition region would alias into the channel edges, mixing neighbouring channels' signals into each other's useful band. Oversampling by 4/3 costs 33 % more samples but keeps every channel's useful band clean, so channels can later be treated as independent.

Method: polyphase form

With fc,j − fLO = (kj + β)·fs/K (β = ½ here: band edges on the K-grid) and the prototype h(r) centred at G = (M−1)/2, zero-padded to L = P·K:

yj(m) = e−i2π(kj+β)(mD+G)/K · Σp e+i2πkjp/K·um(p),   um(p) = Σq hb(p+qK)·x(mD+G−p−qK)

with hb(r) = h(r)·e+i2πβr/K. In words: weight a frame of L samples by the prototype, fold it into K bins, do one K-point inverse FFT for all channels, and correct the phase from the absolute sample index (in double). It is the same arithmetic as K separate mix–filter–decimate receivers at about 1/K of the cost. Prototype: a Kaiser-windowed sinc (same design as the IQ filter), cutoff fs/(2D), 80 dB stopband → 3853 taps (16 per branch).

Tests (tests/testChannelizeIQ.m)

  • Tones at, beside and far from channel centres: every sample equals the exact prediction to ≤ 2.7·10⁻⁷ (single precision). One test pins down frequency mapping, LO phase, timing and gain at once. Passband |H| = 1 ± 1.2·10⁻⁴, stopband −80.2 dB.
  • White noise: channel power / Σh² = 1.0007; passband level 1.0004 of expected, flat within ±3 %.
  • Real data (seed 43, −5 dB): Σ channel band powers / IQ band power = 1.00007 (Parseval: no gaps, no double counting). 0.1 s → 128 × 416,667 samples in 4.1 s (427 MB).

5b · dedisperseChannels validated

info_dc = dedisperseChannels(info_chan, outBase, DM, 'RefFreq', refFreq)

Full interface and help text of dedisperseChannels →

Purpose

Coherent dedispersion of every channel, all channels aligned to one common reference frequency (default 1.6 GHz, the top of the band).

How

Each channel file is an ordinary IQ file, so the validated applyInverseDispersion runs on it with:

  • band = channel centre ± ChanWidth/2: the chirp filter is zero outside, which removes the channelizer's transition region, so the channels tile the band exactly;
  • RefFreq = one common frequency with AllowRefOutsideBand: each channel's chirp includes its delay to the reference, so all channels come out aligned at 1.6 GHz, with no separate shift step;
  • taper (EdgeFrac 0.02 of the channel) inside each channel: neighbours do not overlap, so their noise is independent. ∫W² = 390.0 MHz in total, the same as the full-band path.
Why reuse applyInverseDispersion

It was already validated, including its leakage check and fully-supported range. The only change needed was the AllowRefOutsideBand option, and with it off the full-band output is bit-identical. One validated kernel, two front ends.

Memory per channel: the kernel spans that channel's sweep to the reference at 4.17 MHz (the bottom channel at DM 100 ~5·10⁵ samples, ~100 MB). DM 5: FFT 2¹⁴…2¹⁸, max leakage 6·10⁻⁸, 128 channels in 1.7 s.

Outputs

Info: chanFiles, chanFreqs, chanWidth, fs, N, t0, DM, refFreq, edgeWidth, per-channel nPast/nFuture/Nfft/leakage, fullySupported (valid in every channel), BnoiseChan = 3.0596 MHz and BnoiseTotal = 128 × that = 391.6 MHz (as the full band), bulkDelayRef.

Tests (tests/testDedisperseChannels.m)

  1. Regression: the full-band applyInverseDispersion with defaults is bit-identical to test_IQ_dedispersed.dat.
  2. Commutation (the precise test): channelize(full-band dedispersed) vs dedisperseChannels(channelize(IQ)). Both are linear filters, so the order must not matter. Correlation ≥ 0.999993, phase ≤ 1.2·10⁻⁵ rad, power 1 ± 3·10⁻⁵ (channels 4–125). This proves the per-channel chirps, inter-channel delays and phases are exact.
  3. End to end: TOAs channel path vs full-band path on the same data (seed 43, −5 dB): 8 TOAs, mean +0.19 µs, rms 0.51 µs (0.18σ). The paths keep slightly different parts of the same noise (correlation 0.979), so differences scatter by σ·√(2(1−ρ)) = 0.205σ; χ² 5.98 for 8 → consistent.
  4. Time-bin noise of the summed channel power: variance / radiometer 0.884 (predicted 0.888 ± 0.005), lag-1 correlation 0.054 (0.052 ± 0.004). This revealed the narrow-channel effect below.

powerCovariance: exact noise of detected time bins validated

noise = powerCovariance(chanWidth, edgeWidth, fs, nPerBin, ...)

Full interface and help text of powerCovariance →

The problem it solves

The radiometer equation var = m²/(B·Δt) with independent time bins assumes B·Δt ≫ 1. In the full band B·Δt ≈ 400. In a 3.125 MHz channel with 0.96 µs bins it is only ~3: the detected power has a correlation time ~1/Bch ≈ 0.33 µs, comparable to the bin. So each bin's variance is lower than the formula (0.888 of it) and neighbouring bins are correlated (+0.046 at lag 1).

The exact formula

A bin is the mean of |y|² over n samples. For Gaussian y with normalized autocorrelation R (Isserlis, see physics), relative to the radiometer value:

V = C(0)/rad,   XL = C(L)/rad,   C(L) = (1/n²)·Σa,b|R(a − b + Ln)|²,   rad = Σl|R(l)|²/n

Summed over all lags, V + 2ΣXL = 1 exactly: the independent-bin model is right for long sums, wrong bin by bin. The spectrum is W² of the per-channel taper (the chirp is all-pass and drops out). Lmax = the smallest lag with V + 2Σ≤LX ≥ 1 − 0.002.

BinsVX(1), X(2), X(3)Lmax
3.125 MHz channel, n = 4 (0.96 µs)0.88770.0458, 0.0051, 0.0020 …7
3.125 MHz channel, n = 160.96250.0173 …2
Full band, n = 8000.99867·10⁻⁴1

The slow 1/L² tail comes from the sharp channel edges.

Why carry this exactly instead of ignoring it

Within one phase bin the two effects nearly cancel (variance 0.987 of the simple model), so it looks negligible. But error bars and false-alarm thresholds must be exact at weak signal, phase bins can be shorter for other pulsars, and the same machinery is needed anyway for blanking, where the effect is large (±45 %). So V and X(L) travel with the data through the fold (NoiseCoeffs).

6b · detectChannels validated

info_det = detectChannels(info_dc, outFile, f_out)

Full interface and help text of detectChannels →

Runs the validated detectPower on every dedispersed channel at rate fs/round(fs/fout) (a whole number of channel samples per bin: 4 samples, 0.96 µs, 1.0417 MHz; one warning if it differs from the requested 1 MHz), and interleaves the results block-wise into one float32 [nChan × nBins] file, the layout foldProfile already reads. Temporaries are deleted.

Info: detectPower's fields used by the fold (file, nChan, N, binTime0, binDt, fullySupportedBins, byteOrder, chanFreqs, …) plus chanWidth, chanMeanPower, BnoiseChan, BnoiseTotal and noise (the powerCovariance struct: the same for every channel and bin without blanking). 0.5 s for 0.1 s of 128 channels.

Why a whole number of samples per bin

A bin boundary inside a sample would need interpolation and break the exact noise model. 4 samples at 4.17 MHz = 0.96 µs is close to the 1 µs of the full-band path.

Tests (tests/testDetectChannels.m)

  1. powerCovariance vs the statistics of the actual dedispersion impulse response of channel 1: identical (max |ΔX| 6·10⁻¹⁰).
  2. Rows of the interleaved file bit-identical to detectPower per channel.
  3. foldProfile on the 128-channel file: channel sum = fold of the summed power (1.2·10⁻⁸).
  4. Measured on seed-43 data (68,803 off-pulse bins × 128 channels): V 0.8866 (predicted 0.8877 ± 0.0006), X₁₋₃ 0.0455 / 0.0053 / 0.0018 (0.0458 / 0.0051 / 0.0020 ± 0.0004); variance of 5-bin sums 0.9685 × 5·rad (predicted 0.9692 ± 0.0014; the independent-bin model says 1).

Result: channel path vs full band (A4, 8 Oct)

Seed 43, −5 dB: full band unchanged (9 TOAs, 2.880 µs, SNR 93.4); channels with optimal weighting 8 TOAs, 2.943 µs, SNR 92.3, reduced χ² 1.008, all detected, noise ratio 0.990, total offset +0.12 ± 0.98 µs (full band −0.21 ± 0.96). Per sub-int differences 0.26σ rms. The channel path has one TOA fewer because its fully-supported range is shorter (channelizer and per-channel filter reach).
