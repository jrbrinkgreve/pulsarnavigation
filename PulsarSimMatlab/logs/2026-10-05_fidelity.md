## Work done

- Discussion: how physically accurate is the pipeline, and how relevant is interstellar
  scintillation? Conclusions recorded in `current_project_notes.md` §9 (no code changes,
  no MATLAB runs). In short:
  - Exact (and validated) for what it models over 0.1–1 s: AM-noise signal statistics,
    cold-plasma dispersion + coherent dedispersion, receiver chain, fold, FFTFIT errors.
  - Simplified: white spectrum (real α ≈ −1.6), single stable Gaussian profile, ideal
    single-pol receiver.
  - SNR regime: −5 dB is a bright pulsar on a large dish; a 3 m antenna gives roughly
    −27 dB (Vela) to ≲ −50 dB (typical MSP).
  - Missing and dominant on navigation timescales: Doppler (~100 µs/s), DM variations
    (~2 µs per 1e-3 pc cm⁻³), clock, scintillation.
  - Scintillation at DM 5: τ_d ≈ 0.4 ns, Δν_d ~ 0.1–1 GHz → strong flux fading (SNR
    budget), negligible timing effect; at DM ≳ 100 scattering gives µs timing noise.
    Within a ≤ 1 s voltage sim it is a static random gain g(f); long-term fading belongs
    at TOA/SNR level.

## Decisions

- Keep the voltage pipeline as it is; scintillation not added now. If added later: a
  static g(f) in the forward filter (+ optional multipath tail).
- Phase D sweep range should extend below −30 dB (realistic small-antenna regime).
