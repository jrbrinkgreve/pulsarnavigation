## Design note (agreed)

Purpose: a realistic radio environment for the reference hardware (3×3 array, T_sys ≈ 70 K,
ground station) to measure how many channels get lopsided noise (partly covered by
noise-like RFI) and what that does to χ² and the TOA error bars.

**Correction of 8 Oct.** "χ² 1.04 → TOA error bars ~2 % too small" took χ² at face value.
Every channel counts equally in χ², but the 'optimal' fit weights channel c by
F_c ∝ 1/a_c² (its level). If channel c's noise is r_c × the model's, the reported TOA error
is too small by √(Σ F_c r_c / Σ F_c) (independent channels, weights as used). Lopsided
channels contain the RFI, so their level is high and their weight small: for the 8 Oct LTE
case ~0.1 %, not 2 %. The χ² flag (MaxRedChi2 = 2) is the other practical consequence.

**Lopsided channel, flat-channel formula.** RFI with PSD R × the noise PSD over a fraction f
of the channel: level L = 1 + fR, r = (1 − f + f(1 + R)²) / L², and the error-bar weight
F(r − 1) = f(1 − f) R² / (1 + fR)⁴: largest at R = 1/f (RFI power = the channel's noise),
value (1 − f)/(16 f). Stronger RFI: r → 1/f but the weight → 0. So the worst case for the
error bars is a **narrow** noise-like carrier about as strong as its channel's noise.

**Link budget.** Receiver noise kT_sys·B = −124 dBW (70 K, 400 MHz); INR relative to it.
Array: ~22 dBi main beam (~15°), ~0–3 dBi to the rest of the sky, −10 dBi assumed to the
horizon. GNSS: ICD minimum received powers + ~3 dB, summed over visible satellites
(L1 ~30 → Gaussian by the CLT → `'noise'`; weaker bands one `'bpsk'` each). Other levels:
link-budget estimates, ±10 dB. Radar 1 +40 dB = some tens of km away with terrain in
between (a nearby radar is much stronger and would clip a real ADC: not modelled).

## Run 2 (Claude; cases 1, 13, 14, 15 with the periodic mask; 632 s) — new problem found

Cases 1 and 13 identical to run 1 (no emitter found). But the realistic cases:

| case (with excision + periodic mask) | blanked | good | SNR | χ² | periodic emitters found |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 14 realistic | **29.1 %** | 8/8 | 69.3 (run 1: 82.0) | 1.19 | 351.000 Hz (radar 2, correct), 14108.525 Hz, 14108.424 Hz |
| 15 realistic, Inmarsat narrow | **32.2 %** | 8/8 | 66.2 | 1.40 | as 14 + 14467.613 Hz |

Diagnosis (scratch script on case 15's channel files: detectRFI + periodicRFI, verbose):
- 14108.5 Hz (70.88 µs, two emitters): group of 707 "pulses" in channels 29–94 = radar 1's
  sidelobe pulses + hundreds of false flags from the LTE edge channels 81 / 94. 70.88 µs is
  exactly 2/29 of radar 1's period (1027.7 µs = 14.5 × 70.88 µs): every second radar pulse is
  on the grid and random flags fill in; 67 of 707 fit, logChance −19.9 → accepted. Radar 1
  itself (973 Hz) is never found: with ~7000 flags/s in its group, a pulse's next 3 pulses
  (the candidate gaps) are random flags, not the next radar pulse.
- 14467.6 Hz (69.12 µs = exactly 288 channel samples): 2240 dense false flags in the narrow-
  carrier channels 104–114, 86 fit (~70 expected for uniform random times), logChance −14.2.
  Probably detectRFI's window grid (event times on multiples of the stride) makes random
  times non-uniform in phase for periods that are multiples of the stride, so the binomial
  chance q = (2 TimeTol + 1)/P is too small.
- Each spurious emitter blanks every predicted pulse (width 3.8–9.6 µs every ~70 µs) in its
  channel range ± FreqGuard: channels 22–121, 32 % of all samples.
- **This is main.m's default path (`periodicMask = true`).** Without the periodic mask (run 1)
  the same scenario lost 0.29 %. Root cause of the dense flags = the lopsided channels
  (same as the χ² issue); periodicRFI's significance test assumes sparse, uniformly random
  pulse times, and nothing checks physical plausibility (duty 3–14 %, PRF 14 kHz accepted;
  PeriodRange allows up to 20 kHz).
- The per-channel r check is not valid under heavy blanking (it compares with the median
  channel, not with the blanking-aware model): run 2's "error bars −9 %" and ch 19 r 0.53 are
  artefacts of that, not physics.

## periodicRFI diagnosis, confirmed (after the commit; scratch copy with prints, case 15 data)

- All pulse times lie on a **4-sample grid** (event centre (first + last)/2 of detectRFI's
  windows at strides 1, 1, 2, 4, 8: time mod 8 = 0.5 or 4.5 for 2239 of 2240 pulses in the
  Inmarsat group). For P = 288 = 72 × 4 the best phase held **m = 138** pulses where the
  binomial with q = (2·TimeTol + 1)/P = 9/288 expects 70 → log tail −28.7 → lp −14.2. With
  times on a grid of g samples a pulse fits with chance (2·floor(TimeTol/g) + 1)·g/P: for
  g = 4, 12/P, not 9/P (and the best of ~72 phases). Same group: P = 312 = 78 × 4, lp −10.0.
- 14108.5 Hz (P = 295.33 samples): m 68 of 707, log tail −34.8: a real alias. Radar 1's
  period 4282.3 samples = 14.5 × 295.33, so every second radar pulse is on this grid. The
  true period is never a candidate: the candidates are the gaps to the next 3 pulses, and
  with flags every ~424 samples (median gap) those are flags. Its predicted pulses: 1411, of
  which only 67 have a detection (4.7 %); the 14.5 kHz one 86 of 1447 (5.9 %); radar 2
  (correct) 19 of 35 (54 %).
- Radar 2 found correctly (351.000 Hz, 20 of 57, lp −91.8) in a group without dense flags.
